Painting is one of the easiest trades to get leads for and one of the hardest to get good leads for. Everybody needs a room painted eventually, and everybody gets three quotes. The painters who grow aren't the ones with the most leads. They're the ones with the right mix of sources, and a quick way to sort the whole-house exterior from the "can you paint one bedroom this Saturday" call.

## Rank your lead sources by quality, not volume

Before spending money anywhere, it helps to know how different sources tend to behave for painters:

| Source | Lead quality | Cost | Speed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Referrals and past customers | Highest | Lowest | Slow, steady |
| Google Business Profile | High | Your time | Weeks to build |
| Yard signs with QR codes | High (neighbors) | Low | Seasonal |
| Local Services Ads | Medium-high | Per lead | Fast |
| Nextdoor | Medium-high | Free to low | Variable |
| Meta (Facebook/Instagram) ads | Mixed | Per click/impression | Fast |

The rest of this post goes through each one.

## Google Business Profile

For most painters, Google Business Profile is the single most valuable free source. When someone searches "house painters near me," the map pack shows three businesses. Being one of them matters.

What to focus on:

- **Primary category:** Painter. List interior, exterior, cabinet painting, deck staining and commercial work as separate Services.
- **Photos:** Before-and-afters from every job. Painting is visual, and homeowners scroll photos before calling.
- **Reviews:** Ask every customer the day the job wraps, with a direct link by text.
- **Posts:** Seasonal reminders, like exterior booking in early spring and interior work in winter.

Our [Google Business Profile checklist for contractors](/blog/google-business-profile-checklist-contractors/) covers every setting.

## Referrals and repaint reminders

Your past customers are your cheapest leads, and most painters barely touch them. Two things to build:

**A referral habit.** At the final walkthrough, when the customer is happiest, ask directly: "Do you know anyone else thinking about painting this year?" Follow up with a text they can forward. If you offer a referral thank-you, keep it simple and keep it separate from review requests, since Google doesn't allow incentives for reviews.

**Repaint reminders.** Exterior paint doesn't last forever, and interiors get repainted when people move, renovate or have kids. Set reminders in your CRM:

- Interior customers: a check-in 12 to 18 months later about the next room
- Exterior customers: a check-in several years out, timed for early spring
- Everyone: a seasonal email showing recent work

A [past-customer reactivation campaign](/blog/reactivate-past-customers-text-campaign/) is a good way to start if you've never done this.

## Yard signs with QR codes

Exterior jobs are billboards. Neighbors walk by every day for a week watching the house transform. A yard sign turns that attention into calls.

Make them work harder:

- **Ask the homeowner's permission** and check your town's sign rules.
- **Add a QR code** that links to a page on your website, not your homepage, with a short estimate form.
- **Tag the link** so your CRM records that the lead came from a yard sign (and ideally which street).
- **Leave it up a week or two after the job**, if the homeowner is okay with it.

Neighbors who call from yard signs are often great leads. They've seen your work in person, and their houses are frequently the same age and style.

## Meta ads (Facebook and Instagram)

Meta can bring volume fast, especially with before-and-after photos and short videos. The tradeoff is that people scrolling Facebook weren't looking for a painter, so leads are less ready to buy.

To get better leads from Meta:

- Use real before-and-after photos, not stock images.
- Target your actual service area, not a huge radius.
- If you use Meta's instant lead forms, add qualifying questions (interior or exterior, timeline, home or business) so you're not chasing every form fill.
- Respond fast. These leads go cold even quicker than search leads.

## Local Services Ads

Where painting is an available category in your area, Local Services Ads appear at the top of Google and charge per lead rather than per click. As of 2026, businesses pass Google's checks to earn a "Google Verified" badge, which replaced the older Google Guaranteed and Screened badges starting in October 2025. Leads come in by phone or message, and responsiveness affects how often you're shown, so answer every one. If you can't, a [missed-call text-back](/blog/missed-call-text-back-setup/) helps cover the gaps.

## Nextdoor

Nextdoor is driven by neighbor recommendations. A free business page lets neighbors recommend you, and recommendations show up when someone asks "anyone know a good painter?" Encourage happy customers to recommend you there, respond to mentions, and post occasional project photos. Nextdoor also sells ads for local businesses, which can be worth testing in neighborhoods you most want to work in.

## Qualifying: interior vs. exterior, residential vs. commercial

Good lead generation is only half the job. Painting leads vary wildly, and your crew's time is your inventory. Sort leads before you drive out to measure.

### Questions to ask every lead

1. Interior, exterior or both?
2. Home or business?
3. Rough scope: how many rooms, or how many stories and roughly how big?
4. When do you want it done?
5. Was the home built before 1978? (This triggers lead-safe work practice requirements for disturbing painted surfaces.)
6. Any special work: cabinets, wallpaper removal, drywall repair, staining?
7. Can you send a few photos?

Build these into your web form and your phone intake so every lead arrives with the same information.

### Route them differently

| Lead type | What matters | Next step |
|---|---|---|
| Residential interior | Rooms, timing, furniture moving | Photo estimate or quick visit |
| Residential exterior | Stories, siding type, condition, season | Site visit, book around weather |
| Commercial | Square footage, occupied space, after-hours work, insurance | Site visit with owner or estimator |
| Small jobs | Single room, touch-ups | Minimum charge, or fill-in schedule |

Exterior work in New England has a hard season, so exterior estimates in fall should often be booked for spring. Interior work fills winter. Commercial bids need certificates of insurance and often have longer decision timelines.

## Track every lead's source

None of this works if you can't tell which source sent which job. Ask "how did you hear about us?" on every form and call, use tracking links on yard signs and ads, and record the source in your CRM. Our post on [tracking where your leads come from](/blog/track-where-your-leads-come-from/) shows how. After a season, you'll know which sources bring whole-house exteriors and which bring one-room touch-ups.
