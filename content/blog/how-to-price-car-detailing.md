Most detailers get asked the same question all day: "How much for a detail?" The honest answer is "it depends," and a price that ignores vehicle size and condition will lose you money on the big, dirty jobs. This guide shows how to build a price from your own time, set size and condition tiers, and put the result in an online quote form.

## Why a flat "starting at" price costs you money

A single price like "Full detail $199" works until a pickup with a bed full of mulch dust and a back seat of dog hair pulls in. You either eat the extra hours or you argue at the car, and both are bad.

The other problem is the opposite. If you price everything for your worst-case vehicle, the owner of a clean compact car sees a high number and calls someone else.

Size and condition are what change your hours, and your hours are your real cost. So price from those two things.

## Step 1: Work out your hourly target first

Before you set any vehicle prices, decide what an hour of your labor has to bring in. Include wages or your own pay, supplies, insurance, water and power, and some room for rework and slow days.

For the examples below, say your target is **$75 per labor hour**. That is a made-up number for illustration. Use your own.

Then time yourself. Track a handful of real jobs by vehicle type, start to finish, for a couple of weeks. Write down actual hours, not what you hoped the job would take.

## Step 2: Set size tiers

Three or four size tiers are plenty. More than that and your quote form turns into a quiz.

Here's a hypothetical table for a full interior and exterior package, using a $75 hourly target:

| Size tier | Example vehicles | Base hours | Base labor |
|---|---|---|---|
| Small | Coupe, compact sedan | 3.0 | $225 |
| Mid | Midsize sedan, small SUV | 3.5 | $262.50 |
| Large | Full-size SUV, crew-cab pickup | 4.5 | $337.50 |
| Extra large | Passenger van, three-row SUV with cargo area | 5.0 | $375 |

Add materials as a flat amount per package or fold them into the hourly rate. Either works. Just pick one and be consistent.

## Step 3: Add condition as a multiplier or as add-ons

Condition is harder to standardize, so make it concrete. Define what "light," "moderate," and "heavy" actually mean in your shop.

- **Light:** regularly cleaned, no stains, no odors, minimal pet hair.
- **Moderate:** a few months of dirt, some stains, light pet hair, kid crumbs.
- **Heavy:** heavy staining, thick pet hair, smoke or spill odor, caked-on exterior grime, neglected paint.

Now pick a time multiplier for each. Say light is 1.0x, moderate is 1.3x, and heavy is 1.6x.

Take the Mid tier (3.5 base hours) at moderate condition: 3.5 × 1.3 = 4.55 hours, and 4.55 × $75 = **$341.25**. The same vehicle in heavy condition is 3.5 × 1.6 = 5.6 hours, or **$420**.

A multiplier is quick, but some conditions are better as fixed add-ons because they're separate tasks. Common ones:

- Pet hair removal
- Seat or carpet extraction for stains
- Odor treatment
- Clay bar and decontamination
- Single-stage polish or paint correction
- Headlight restoration
- Engine bay cleanup

Say pet hair is +1 hour ($75) and a single-stage polish is +3 hours ($225). Write each add-on as its own line item. Customers accept an add-on they can see more easily than a vague "dirty car surcharge."

Paint correction and ceramic coating prep are where hours vary most. If you sell coatings, give them their own quote path rather than forcing them into the basic wash-and-detail form. ## Step 4: Turn it into a quote customers can get online

Once you have the formula, a website can run it for you. A good online quote tool for a detailer needs a short list of inputs:

1. **Vehicle:** year, make, and model, or just a size tier picker with example vehicles.
2. **Package:** interior, exterior, or both.
3. **Condition:** three options with one-line plain-English descriptions, ideally with a "not sure" choice.
4. **Add-ons:** checkboxes for pet hair, stains, odor, polish, and so on.
5. **Photos:** an optional upload of three or four shots (front seat, back seat, cargo area, and one of the paint).
6. **Contact details:** name, mobile number, and preferred day.

The tool then shows a **price range**, not a single hard number. Ranges protect you when a car turns out to be worse than described.

A few design rules matter more than they seem:

- Show the range on the screen right away. Don't hide it behind a "we'll email you" form, or people leave.
- Use plain words for condition. Nobody knows if their car is "moderate."
- Tell people the final price is confirmed when you see the vehicle, and what would change it.
- Send the quote to you with the photos attached, so you can approve or adjust quickly.

## Guardrails: what to put in writing

A price formula only works if you stand behind it consistently. Put these in writing on the quote page and in the confirmation:

- The range is an estimate based on the customer's answers.
- The final price is confirmed at drop-off or arrival.
- What counts as extra: heavy pet hair, biohazards, mold, smoke, or anything not disclosed.
- Your deposit and cancellation terms.

### Check sales tax before you add a line for it

Sales tax on detailing is a place where online advice conflicts. As of 2026, [Mass.gov's sales and use tax guide](https://mass.gov/dor/individuals/taxpayer-help-and-resources/tax-guides/salesuse-tax-guide.html) lists car repairs among services that aren't taxed. A [DOR letter ruling on auto repairs](https://www.mass.gov/letter-ruling/letter-ruling-85-19-auto-repairs) says that when a service business separately states parts on the bill, it collects tax on the parts only. Some detailing sites, however, claim that Massachusetts detailers must collect tax on most services. Detailing isn't repair, and products you sell outright may be treated differently. Confirm your setup with the Department of Revenue or your accountant before you build tax into the quote tool.

### If the tool texts quotes, register your number

Texting quotes from software on a local 10-digit number generally requires A2P 10DLC registration. As of 2026, even low-volume templated messages sent through a platform need it, per [OptInFix's compliance guide](https://optinfix.com/guides/10dlc). Registration also isn't a substitute for customer consent. Get permission before you text, and read our [A2P 10DLC walkthrough for small businesses](/blog/a2p-10dlc-texting-registration-small-business/) before you switch it on.

## What to do after the quote

A quote is only the first step. Follow up the same day, and if the customer doesn't book, send one reminder. If you're building toward repeat work, offer a maintenance plan after the first detail. Our post on [car detailing membership plans](/blog/car-detailing-membership-plans/) shows how to price those so they don't undercut your one-time jobs.

Also, review your numbers every quarter. Pull ten finished jobs, compare estimated hours to actual hours, and adjust the tiers. If your large SUVs consistently run 30 minutes over, change the base hours rather than absorbing it.
