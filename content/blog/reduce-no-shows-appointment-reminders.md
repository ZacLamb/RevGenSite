A no-show isn't just an empty hour. It's an hour you turned someone else away from, a crew that drove across town, or a bay that sat empty while the next customer waited until next week. Most no-shows aren't people blowing you off. They forgot, something came up, or rescheduling felt like more trouble than skipping.

A good reminder sequence fixes most of that. Here's how to build one for wellness practices, detailers and any business that books estimates.

## Why people no-show

Knowing the cause tells you which fix to use:

| Cause | Fix |
|---|---|
| Forgot | Reminders at the right times |
| Booked weeks ago, plans changed | Easy reschedule link |
| Unsure of time, location or prep | Clear details in each reminder |
| Low commitment (free estimate, first visit) | Confirmation step, deposit for high-value slots |
| Felt awkward canceling | Make canceling a one-tap action |

The goal isn't to nag. It's to make showing up, or telling you they can't, as easy as possible.

## A reminder sequence that works

This is a solid starting point for most appointment-based businesses. Adjust the timing to your booking window.

1. **Immediately after booking:** confirmation by text and email with date, time, address, what to bring or prepare, and links to reschedule or cancel.
2. **Two to three days before** (for appointments booked more than a week out): reminder with a "Reply C to confirm" or a confirm button.
3. **The day before:** reminder with the time, location and any prep instructions.
4. **Two hours before** (or the morning of): short reminder. For in-home visits, an "on my way" text when the tech leaves.
5. **If they don't confirm:** a call task for your front desk the day before.

Example texts:

> "Hi Jordan, you're booked for a full interior detail Thursday at 9:00 AM at our Rockland shop. Plan on about 4 hours. Need to change it? [reschedule link]. Reply C to confirm."

> "Reminder: your estimate with Dave from [Company] is tomorrow between 1 and 3 PM. He'll text when he's on the way. Need a different time? [link]"

Keep each one short. One message, one job.

## Confirm and reschedule links

The single most useful thing a reminder can include is a one-tap way to reschedule.

- **Confirm:** "Reply C" or a button that marks the appointment confirmed in your calendar. Unconfirmed appointments get a call.
- **Reschedule:** a link to your booking calendar showing open slots. When someone moves an appointment, the old slot opens for someone else.
- **Cancel:** yes, offer it. A cancellation two days out is far better than a no-show, because you can fill the slot.

Set up a waitlist if you can. When a cancellation comes in, the system texts the next people in line: "A 2:00 PM slot opened up tomorrow. Want it?"

## Deposits and policies

For long or high-value appointments, a deposit changes behavior. It works especially well for:

- Full details, paint correction and ceramic coating (a whole day of bay time)
- Extended wellness sessions, packages or first-visit evaluations
- Specialty estimates that need a senior estimator's half day

Tips for deposits:

- **State the policy at booking,** not in fine print. "A $50 deposit holds your spot and goes toward your service. Reschedule free up to 48 hours before."
- **Keep the rescheduling window generous.** The point is commitment, not penalties.
- **Apply it toward the service.** It shouldn't feel like a fee.

For free estimates, a deposit is usually a bad idea. A confirmation step and a reminder from the estimator by name work better.

## Industry notes

### Healthcare and wellness practices

Chiropractors, physical therapists, dental practices, med spas, massage and acupuncture clinics all live and die by the schedule. A few extra considerations:

- **Privacy.** Patient appointment messages carry privacy obligations under HIPAA and possibly state law. Keep reminders minimal: name, date, time and location, without diagnosis or treatment details. Get documented consent to text, use a texting and CRM setup you've reviewed for privacy, and check your vendor agreements and message content with your compliance advisor before you launch. Don't assume a tool is compliant because its website says so.
- **Recurring visits.** For care plans with weekly visits, send a short reminder before each one, plus a "book your next visit" message after each appointment.
- **Intake forms.** Send forms with the first reminder so patients aren't filling them out in the lobby.

### Auto detailing and restoration

- Include drop-off instructions, how long the vehicle will be there and whether to remove personal items.
- For mobile detailing, confirm the address, water and power access, and where to park.
- Send an "almost done" text so pickups are on time.

### Estimate appointments for trades

For HVAC, remodeling, masonry and similar estimates:

- Name the estimator and include a photo if you can. People open the door for a face they recognize.
- Give a time window and send an "on my way" text.
- Ask them to have the relevant info ready: the model number of the old unit, photos, HOA requirements.

## Write reminders people actually read

A few habits make reminders more effective:

- **Lead with the time and day.** "Tomorrow at 9:00 AM" belongs in the first line, since many people only read the preview.
- **Sign with a real name** or the business name people know you by.
- **Use plain words.** Skip the marketing language; this is a logistics message.
- **Match the channel.** Text for short reminders, email for longer prep instructions or forms.

## Recovering no-shows

Even with good reminders, some will slip. Automate a recovery message 15 to 30 minutes after a missed appointment:

> "Sorry we missed you today, Alex. Things come up. Want to grab another time? [link]"

No guilt, just a link. Follow with one more message a couple of days later. After that, move them to a longer-term follow-up list. Your CRM should track no-shows by person so repeat offenders get a deposit requirement next time.

## Texting rules to know

Reminder texts are business messaging, so the usual rules apply:

- Get consent to text at booking and keep a record of it.
- Honor STOP replies immediately.
- Make sure your business texting number is properly registered. Unregistered numbers get filtered by carriers, which means reminders that never arrive. See our [A2P 10DLC registration guide](/blog/a2p-10dlc-texting-registration-small-business/).
