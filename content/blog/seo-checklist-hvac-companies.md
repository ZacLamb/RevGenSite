HVAC is one of the more competitive local search categories. Jobs are valuable, demand swings with the weather, and every market has several companies fighting for the same map listings. This checklist covers the fundamentals in the order most HVAC companies should tackle them, with the platform rules checked against Google's own documentation as of 2026.

## How Google ranks local results

Google says local results are based mainly on relevance, distance and prominence ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en)). Relevance is how well your profile and site match the search. Distance is how far you are from the searcher. Prominence is how well known you are, which Google says is shaped by things like links to your site and your review count and ratings. Google also states that you can't pay for a better local ranking.

Everything below works on relevance and prominence. Distance you can't change, but a clear service area helps you show up where you actually work.

## 1. Google Business Profile

Your profile is often the first thing a homeowner sees. Get the basics right:

- **Business name exactly as it appears in the real world.** Google's guidelines say adding unnecessary information, such as keywords or locations, to your name isn't permitted ([Google Business Profile guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en)). "Smith Heating & Cooling" is fine. "Smith Heating & Cooling Best AC Repair Worcester" is not.
- **The most specific primary category.** Google advises using as few categories as possible, chosen to be as specific as possible. Pick the one that matches your main work (for example, HVAC contractor), then add only secondary categories that are genuinely core.
- **Hide your address if customers don't come to you.** Google says service-area businesses should hide their address. As of 2026 you can list up to 20 service areas by city or postal code ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9157481?hl=en)).
- **Services and descriptions.** Add each service you offer: furnace repair, boiler service, AC installation, heat pump installation, ductless mini-splits, maintenance plans.
- **Real photos.** Trucks, team, installs, equipment. Keep adding them through the year.
- **Hours that match reality**, including emergency availability if you offer it.
- **Regular posts.** Seasonal tune-up reminders, rebate updates, new equipment lines.

For the full list, see our [Google Business Profile checklist for contractors](/blog/google-business-profile-checklist-contractors/).

## 2. Website structure

Your site needs to answer specific searches with specific pages.

- **One page per service.** Separate pages for AC repair, AC installation, furnace repair, furnace installation, boiler service, heat pumps, ductless systems, indoor air quality and maintenance plans. One "HVAC Services" page can't rank for all of them.
- **Town pages for your core service area.** Each one with real local detail, not a template with the town swapped in. Our post on [local service pages](/blog/why-trade-businesses-need-local-service-pages/) explains how to avoid thin pages.
- **Click-to-call and booking on every page.** Not just the homepage. HVAC searches on a hot or cold day are often urgent and mobile.
- **Clear emergency messaging** if you offer after-hours service.
- **Financing and rebate information** on installation pages, if you offer financing or your utility runs rebate programs. Check current programs before you publish and update the page when they change.

## 3. Page speed and Core Web Vitals

Google's ranking systems use Core Web Vitals. As of 2026, the "good" thresholds are Largest Contentful Paint at 2.5 seconds or less, Interaction to Next Paint at 200 milliseconds or less, and Cumulative Layout Shift at 0.1 or less, measured at the 75th percentile of real visits ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/vitals)). Google is also clear that relevance comes first, and a fast page won't outrank a more relevant one on speed alone ([Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/page-experience)).

In practice: compress photos, cut plugins you don't use, and test on a phone over cellular data. Details in [website speed and local rankings](/blog/website-speed-and-local-rankings/).

## 4. Reviews

Google lists review count and ratings among the things that shape prominence, and notes that responding to reviews shows you value customer feedback. The practical system:

- Send a review request with a direct link right after each job is marked complete.
- Ask every customer, not just the happy ones. Google's policy prohibits selectively soliciting positive reviews and prohibits offering incentives for reviews ([Google Maps content policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114)).
- Reply to every review. Mention the service when it fits naturally ("Glad the new heat pump is keeping up").
- Respond calmly to negative reviews and take the details offline.

More in [turning Google reviews into marketing](/blog/turning-google-reviews-into-marketing/).

## 5. Seasonal content

HVAC demand follows the weather, and so do searches. Publish ahead of each season, not during it:

| When to publish | Topic ideas |
|---|---|
| Late winter | AC tune-up checklist, signs your AC won't make it through summer, heat pump vs. central AC |
| Late spring | What to do when your AC stops cooling, thermostat settings, ductless for upstairs rooms |
| Late summer | Furnace and boiler tune-up checklist, oil-to-heat-pump conversion questions |
| Fall | No-heat troubleshooting, carbon monoxide safety, maintenance plan benefits |

Each article should link to the matching service page. A tune-up article links to your maintenance plan page. A "no heat" article links to furnace and boiler repair.

## 6. Citations and local links

- **Consistent name, phone and service area** across the directories that matter: Google, Bing Places, Apple Business Connect, Yelp, the BBB if you use it, and your manufacturer's dealer locator.
- **Real local links.** Manufacturer dealer pages, supply houses, local chambers of commerce, sponsorships of local teams or events, and trade association listings.
- **Skip paid link schemes.** They're a risk with little upside for a local contractor.

## 7. Track what turns into jobs

Rankings are a means, not the goal. Track calls and form fills from organic search and your profile, and follow them into your CRM to see which pages produce booked installs versus tune-ups. That tells you where to write next. Pair it with a [maintenance plan renewal automation](/blog/hvac-maintenance-plan-renewal-automation/) so the customers SEO brings in keep coming back.

## Putting it together

None of these items changes rankings overnight. Done together and kept up over months, a complete profile, a well-structured fast site, a steady flow of recent reviews and seasonal content add up to a presence that's hard for a competitor to match.
