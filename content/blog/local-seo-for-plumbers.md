When a pipe bursts, the homeowner doesn't browse. They search "emergency plumber near me," call one of the first three results, and hire whoever answers. Local SEO for plumbers is about being one of those three results in every town you serve, and then making sure the call gets answered. Here's the playbook, in the order we'd tackle it.

## How plumbing searches actually play out

A typical plumbing search page has three layers:

1. **Local Services Ads** at the top, paid per lead.
2. **The map pack**, three businesses pulled from Google Business Profiles.
3. **Organic results**, the regular website listings below.

Emergency searches lean heavily on the top two because people are on their phones and calling immediately. Planned work like water heater replacement, repiping or a bathroom rough-in gets more research, so your website and reviews do more of the selling. A good plan covers all three layers instead of betting on one.

## Start with your Google Business Profile

The map pack runs on your Google Business Profile, so this is the fastest place to make gains. The essentials for a plumber:

- Primary category: **Plumber**. Add closely related categories only if they truly describe your work.
- Hide your address if customers don't come to your shop, and list up to 20 service areas (the limit as of 2026).
- List each service separately: drain cleaning, sewer line repair, water heater repair and replacement, tankless installs, sump pumps, gas piping, fixture installs, frozen pipe repair.
- Only claim 24-hour availability if your phone is actually answered at night.

We go through every setting in our [Google Business Profile checklist for contractors](/blog/google-business-profile-checklist-contractors/), including the suspension triggers to avoid.

## Build a page for every service you want calls for

One "Services" page listing everything you do won't rank for much. Each service that brings real revenue deserves its own page. For most plumbing companies that means:

- Drain cleaning and hydro jetting
- Sewer line inspection, repair and replacement
- Water heater repair and replacement (tank and tankless)
- Sump pump installation and repair
- Burst and frozen pipe repair
- Gas line installation
- Repiping
- Backflow testing, if you're certified
- Toilet, faucet and fixture installation

Each page should answer the questions a homeowner asks on the phone: signs of the problem, what you'll do, how long it takes, whether a permit is involved, what affects the price, and how fast you can get there. Add photos from real jobs and a few reviews that mention that service. End with a click-to-call button and a short form.

## Build town pages that aren't copy-paste

Town pages help you show up for "plumber in [town]" searches beyond your immediate area. They only work if each one is useful on its own. Things that make a town page real:

- The neighborhoods or villages you cover in that town
- Common plumbing issues in the local housing stock, like older cast iron drains in pre-war neighborhoods or well systems in rural areas
- Whether the town is on municipal water and sewer or mostly private wells and septic
- Photos and short write-ups of jobs you've done there
- Reviews from customers in that town

A page that only swaps the town name into the same 400 words is easy for Google to spot and does little. Our post on [why trade businesses need local service pages](/blog/why-trade-businesses-need-local-service-pages/) covers the structure in more detail.

## Target emergency keywords honestly

Emergency searches are the most valuable plumbing traffic, and the most competitive. The terms worth building around include "emergency plumber," "burst pipe repair," "no hot water," "sewer backup," "water heater leaking," and "24 hour plumber," paired with your towns.

To compete for them:

- Build a dedicated emergency plumbing page with your response area and a large tap-to-call button at the top.
- Put the phone number in the header on every page, as a tap-to-call link on mobile.
- Say plainly what counts as an emergency and what to do while waiting (where to find the main shutoff, when to call the gas company first).
- Only use "24/7" language if it's true. Ranking for "24 hour plumber" and then sending callers to voicemail burns money and reviews.

If you can't staff nights, a [missed-call text-back](/blog/missed-call-text-back-setup/) or an AI answering setup at least makes sure the caller hears from you within a minute instead of calling the next plumber.

## Make reviews a daily habit

For plumbers, reviews do two jobs: they influence map rankings, and they're what a stressed homeowner reads in the ten seconds before choosing who to call. A system beats a push:

1. Mark the job complete in your CRM or field software.
2. The customer automatically gets a text with your direct Google review link.
3. If they don't leave one, they get one reminder a few days later.
4. You reply to every review, mentioning the service and town naturally.

Don't offer anything in exchange for reviews, and don't filter unhappy customers away from Google. Both are against Google's policies as of 2026. For more ideas, see [turning Google reviews into marketing](/blog/turning-google-reviews-into-marketing/).

## Clean up your citations

Citations are listings of your business name, address and phone number on other sites. They matter less than they used to, but inconsistent ones still confuse Google and customers. Check and correct these first:

- Bing Places and Apple Business Connect (they feed Bing, Apple Maps and Siri)
- Yelp, BBB, Angi, Nextdoor and Facebook
- Your state license lookup, if it shows a business name and phone
- Your local chamber of commerce or trade association directory

Use exactly the same business name and phone number everywhere. If you moved or changed numbers, hunt down the old listings, since they keep sending calls to the wrong place.

## Where Local Services Ads fit

Local Services Ads aren't SEO, but they sit right above your SEO results, so they belong in the plan. As of 2026, you pay per lead rather than per click, and Google rolled its older trust badges into a single "Google Verified" badge starting in October 2025 ([Search Engine Journal coverage](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-confirms-new-google-verified-badge-for-local-services-ads/554360/)). Ranking is influenced by your reviews, proximity and how responsive you are to leads, and missed calls can hurt that responsiveness.

| Channel | What you pay for | How fast it works | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Google Business Profile | Your time | Weeks | Map pack calls, especially emergencies |
| Service and town pages | Writing and web work | Months, then compounding | Planned jobs and towns beyond your base |
| Local Services Ads | Each lead | Days after verification | Filling gaps while SEO builds |
| Google Search Ads | Each click | Days | Specific high-value jobs you want more of |

For a deeper comparison, read [Google Local Services Ads vs. Google Ads](/blog/google-local-services-ads-vs-google-ads/).

## Don't forget site speed and tracking

Two last items tie it together. A slow site loses emergency callers before the page loads, and our post on [website speed and local rankings](/blog/website-speed-and-local-rankings/) explains what to check. And you need to know which channel each call came from, using tracking numbers or form source fields in your CRM. Without that, you can't tell whether SEO, LSAs or referrals are paying for themselves.
