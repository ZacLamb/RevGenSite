When a homeowner sees haze between the panes of a double-pane window, they usually don't know what failed or who fixes it. They type something like "foggy window repair near me" and call whoever looks like they handle it. This guide covers what to put on your site, your Google profile, and your ads so that company is you.

## What homeowners actually mean when they search

Most people who search for foggy window repair have a failed seal on an insulated glass unit (IGU). They may not use that term. They say "fogged," "cloudy," "condensation between the glass," or "window is steamed up."

Your marketing has to catch all of those phrases and sort out the fake leads. Fog on the room side or outside is a humidity or weather issue, and only fog between the panes points to a broken seal.

| What the homeowner says | What it probably is | What they need from you |
|---|---|---|
| "Haze between the panes" | Failed IGU seal | Glass-only replacement or a quote |
| "Window sweats inside in winter" | Indoor humidity | Honest advice, maybe no job |
| "Whole window won't close and is foggy" | Frame or sash problem plus seal | Window repair or full replacement |
| "Foggy window under warranty?" | Warranty claim | Help reading the label and filing |
| "Defog kit or DIY fix?" | Researching | A page that explains the tradeoffs |

If your site only says "window repair," you miss most of these searches.

## Build one page that answers the real question

Make a dedicated page for foggy windows. Don't bury it as a bullet on a general "windows" page. Homeowners should be able to confirm in 20 seconds that you do this job.

Cover these points in plain language:

1. **How to tell it's a failed seal.** Fog or film between the panes that wipes off nowhere. Add a line on room-side and outside condensation so the wrong callers self-select out.
2. **What the fix is.** Glass-only replacement is usually the best choice when the frame is square and the sash works properly. The seal itself can't be repaired.
3. **What you say about defogging.** If you don't offer it, say why. These services drill a small hole, remove the moisture and reseal it, but the failed seal is still compromised and fog can return. They also don't restore an argon fill. If you do offer it, be honest about those limits.
4. **When a whole window makes more sense.** Rotted frame, sash that won't operate, or a homeowner who wants an upgrade.
5. **What matching means.** Ask for the new unit to match the original Low-E coating and tint, so it looks consistent next to the neighboring windows. Tell customers you do this.
6. **How to get a quote fast.** A form that takes photos, plus your phone number. The [photo estimate approach for glass repair](/blog/photo-estimate-glass-repair/) walks through this.

Add a short list of the towns you serve, with a sentence or two of real local detail each. See our [glass and glazing web design](/industries/glass-glazing/web-design/) page for the types of pages this trade needs.

## Set up Google Business Profile for a service-area job

Foggy window work happens at the customer's house, so you're likely a service-area or hybrid business. A few rules from Google's help page, as of 2026:

- You can set up to 20 service areas, based on cities, postal codes, or other areas you serve.
- You can't define your service area as a radius around your business.
- A service-area business can have only one profile for the whole area it serves.

Source: [Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9157481?hl=en).

Beyond that:

- Pick the primary category that matches where most of your revenue comes from, and test the category names in the profile editor. Add secondary categories only for work you really do.
- List foggy window and IGU replacement as its own service, with a one-line description.
- Upload real photos: before and after of a clouded unit, a label check, a crew measuring.
- Ask for reviews that name the job. "They replaced the fogged glass in our bay window" helps more than "great service." Our guide on [turning Google reviews into marketing](/blog/turning-google-reviews-into-marketing/) has the steps.

## Paid search and Local Services Ads

Foggy windows are not usually a 2 a.m. emergency, so people compare. That favors being visible in more than one spot.

**Local Services Ads.** Google says Local Services Ads are only available for certain service categories, and its US list includes window repair services. Availability depends on your area, so check eligibility in your own market before you plan around it. Our comparison of [Google Local Services Ads vs. Google Ads](/blog/google-local-services-ads-vs-google-ads/) covers how to decide.

**Search ads.** If you run standard Google Ads, group keywords by intent:

- Replace-intent: "foggy window repair," "replace fogged glass," "window seal failure," "IGU replacement."
- Add negative keywords for people who won't hire you, such as "DIY," "kit," "how to," "windshield," and "car."
- Send each ad group to the foggy window page, not your home page.

Do the math before you spend. Say your average foggy-unit job is $400 and you're willing to spend 20% of it on marketing. That's $80 per booked job. If one in four calls books, you can pay up to $20 per call. Those numbers are made up, so plug in your own.

## Use the warranty angle

A lot of failed seals are covered. The National Fenestration Rating Council says insulating-glass failure warranties commonly range from 10 to 20 years, as reported by Parade. Coverage varies by manufacturer, and one contractor source notes the glass warranty typically doesn't cover labor. Another contractor source says seal failure is a manufacturer warranty claim rather than a homeowners insurance claim. Treat these as general guidance and tell customers to read their own paperwork.

Marketing ideas that follow from this:

- Add a "Is my window still under warranty?" section to the foggy page, with a short checklist: find the manufacturer label, note the install date, call the manufacturer before replacing anything.
- Offer to inspect the label and tell the homeowner who to call. Only promise this if you'll really do it.
- Be clear about what you charge for labor if the glass is covered.

This builds trust. Even when a homeowner files the claim and you don't get the glass, you're often the one who installs it.

## Reach people who haven't searched yet

A failed seal can show up before the fog does. A seal can fail without visible signs in the early stages. South- and west-facing windows usually fail first because the glass flexes more with heat through the day.

If you've installed or replaced windows before, you already have a list. A short text or email to past customers, something like "If you notice haze between the panes, reply with a photo and we'll tell you if it's the seal," costs little. Check consent and texting registration first. Our post on [reactivating past customers by text](/blog/reactivate-past-customers-text-campaign/) covers the setup.

Property managers and home inspectors are another source. Home inspection standards don't require inspectors to report on failed window seals, so buyers and agents often find these after closing.
