## The problem in painting

A painting estimate request is easy to lose. The homeowner who wants their living room and hallway done calls on a Tuesday afternoon, while you're two hours into prepping a Victorian's trim. The call goes to voicemail. They call the next company on the list, who answers, and they never hear your callback.

The bigger losses are quieter:

- **Estimate requests outrun your capacity.** In spring a small shop can get more requests than one estimator can measure in a week. Without triage, the first callers get seen and everyone else waits.
- **Proposals go cold.** You measure, price the prep and coats, send a clean proposal, and then nobody follows up because the next walkthrough is in twenty minutes.
- **The office work piles up at night.** Writing up proposals, confirming start dates, sending color sheets and requesting reviews all happen after the crews go home.
- **Past customers drift away.** The family whose exterior you did would happily book you for interiors this winter, if anyone asked.

## What we build for painting companies

Each piece below is a working automation, not a slide in a pitch deck. We build them around how your shop actually sells.

1. **An AI call and chat agent for estimate requests.** It answers when you can't, asks the questions your estimator would ask (interior or exterior, which rooms or surfaces, rough square footage, timeline, address), requests photos by text, and books a walkthrough on your calendar. Anything it can't handle goes to you as an instant text alert or a booked callback.
2. **Missed-call text back.** If a call isn't answered, the caller gets a text within moments offering to book an estimate, which keeps them from dialing the next painter.
3. **Proposal follow-up sequences.** After an estimate goes out, timed texts and emails check in, answer common objections about prep and product choice, and stop as soon as the customer responds.
4. **Proposal and job-sheet generation.** Measurements and selections entered after a walkthrough become a formatted proposal, and the signed proposal becomes a crew job sheet with rooms, products, colors and sheen.
5. **Color consultation and start-date reminders.** Booking links for color meetings, sample reminders, and a heads-up to move furniture and clear the driveway before the crew arrives.
6. **Review requests after the final walkthrough.** Sent once the job is marked complete and paid, while the customer is still looking at fresh walls.
7. **Off-season reactivation.** Exterior customers get an interior offer in late fall, and interior customers get a note about exterior scheduling before spring.

## How it works

1. **Audit.** We map every repetitive step from first call to final payment and flag where estimates are slipping away.
2. **Design.** We script the AI agent around your services, your pricing approach and the way you talk to customers.
3. **Build and connect.** We wire everything into your CRM, phone line and calendar so walkthroughs land where your estimator already looks.
4. **Tune and support.** We watch the first 60 days closely, read the conversations and adjust scripts and timing as we see real calls come through.

## What to look for (even if you don't hire us)

If you're comparing AI or automation vendors for a painting business, check:

- Does the agent refuse to quote firm prices, and instead collect measurements and photos for a real estimate?
- Can you read every call transcript and chat, and change the script yourself?
- Is there a clear handoff to a human for commercial jobs, unhappy customers or anything unusual?
- Do follow-up sequences stop automatically when the customer replies, or will they keep texting someone who already signed?
- Is your business texting number registered for A2P 10DLC (as of 2026, carriers expect this for business texting in the US)? Our guide to [A2P 10DLC registration](/blog/a2p-10dlc-texting-registration-small-business/) explains why it matters.
- Will automated messages respect quiet hours, so nobody gets a proposal reminder at 10 p.m.?
