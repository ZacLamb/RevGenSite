## The problem with most restoration and detailing websites

Restoration and detailing sell completely differently, yet most shop sites cover both with one generic page.

- **Restoration needs trust built over months.** A frame-off restoration is a long, expensive commitment. Prospects often spend weeks looking at your past work before they call. A gallery of six glossy finished photos doesn't show them how you handle rust, sourcing or wiring.
- **Detailing needs booking in minutes.** A detailing customer comparing three shops on a Tuesday night will book whichever one lets them pick a time and package without calling.
- **Packages are a mystery.** "Gold, Silver, Bronze" means nothing. Buyers comparing ceramic coatings or PPF want to know the prep, the coverage and the aftercare.
- **No-shows and tire-kickers fill the bay calendar.** Without deposits on big jobs, a cancelled coating appointment leaves a bay empty for a day or more.
- **Social does the selling, the site does nothing.** Many shops post incredible builds on Instagram, then send people to a website that doesn't show any of it.

## What we build for restoration and detailing shops

1. **Project pages for restoration builds.** One page per car with the story: what came in, what was found, the work done, and photos at each stage. Organized so a visitor can browse by era, make or type of work.
2. **Service pages that split the business.** Restoration, rust and metal work, paint, mechanical and electrical, paint correction, ceramic coating, PPF, and interior detailing each get their own page, written for the buyer of that service.
3. **Package pages for coatings and film.** Clear breakdowns of what each tier includes and who it suits, with real photos from your bay.
4. **Online booking with deposits.** A booking flow that lets detailing clients choose a package and time, with an optional deposit on larger services and automatic confirmations.
5. **Restoration inquiry form.** A longer form for build inquiries that asks year, make, model, condition, goals and timeline, plus photo uploads, so your first conversation is a real one.
6. **Town pages for your detailing area.** Detailing is a local service, so we build a page for each town you draw from, with mobile or drop-off details where relevant.
7. **Show and event section.** A simple place to list car shows and meets you'll attend and feature cars you've worked on that are showing, which matters to an enthusiast audience.

## What to look for (even if you don't hire us)

Use these questions with any designer:

- **Can you add a new project page yourself, or quickly through them?** Your portfolio should grow with every car.
- **How are the galleries built?** Large photo galleries need image compression and lazy loading, or the site crawls on a phone.
- **Can a detailing client book without calling?** Try it on your own phone, start to finish.
- **Are deposits possible on high-value services?** Ask how payments connect and where the money lands.
- **Is restoration separated from detailing?** They're different buyers and need different pages.
- **Who writes the copy?** Ask whether they'll interview you about your process or just fill in a template.
