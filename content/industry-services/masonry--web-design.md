## The problem in masonry

Masonry has an odd marketing gap. The work itself is some of the most photogenic in the trades: a granite step set true, a fieldstone wall with tight joints, a rebuilt chimney with fresh flashing. Yet most of those photos live in the owner's camera roll, with a few posted to Facebook and then buried.

That matters because masonry buyers shop slowly and visually. A homeowner pricing a paver patio or retaining wall is buying something that will sit in their yard for decades, and they pick largely on whose finished work looks like what they want. A logo and a stock photo of bricks loses to a site with twelve real patios on it.

There is also a search problem. People type specific things: "chimney repointing near me," "stone retaining wall contractor," "granite steps installer" plus a town name. A one-page site cannot rank for all of those. And when a real estimate request does come in through a generic contact form, it often arrives with no address, no photos and no sense of size, so you burn a site visit finding out it was a 6-foot walkway repair.

## What we build for masonry contractors

- **Service pages for each type of work.** Patios and walkways, retaining walls, chimneys, steps and stoops, stone veneer, outdoor fireplaces. Each one covers your method, materials and the questions buyers ask, like when a chimney needs a rebuild instead of repointing.
- **Project pages, not just a gallery grid.** Each featured job gets its own short write-up: the town, what the homeowner wanted, the stone or block used, and before and after photos. This is the kind of page that wins a comparison shopper. Our post on [building a project gallery for hardscaping and masonry](/blog/hardscaping-masonry-project-gallery-website/) goes deeper on how to shoot and organize them.
- **Town pages for your service area.** A page for each town you work in, with the kinds of jobs you do there and local projects where you have them, so you show up when someone searches with a town name.
- **An estimate request form built for big jobs.** Fields for job type, rough dimensions, address, timeline and a photo upload, so you can triage before you drive out.
- **Click-to-call and site visit booking** on every page.
- **A fast, mobile-first build.** Big photo galleries slow sites down, so images are sized and compressed properly and the code stays lean.

## What to look for (even if you don't hire us)

- Can the designer give each service its own page, with its own photos, rather than one "Services" page with a bulleted list?
- Will they write project pages with real detail, or just drop photos into a slider?
- Does the estimate form ask for the address, the job type and photos?
- Do they compress images and test load speed on a phone?
- Who owns the domain, the hosting account and the photos when you part ways?
- Will the site have a page for each town you serve, written for that town rather than with the name swapped in?
