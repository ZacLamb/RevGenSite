## The problem in HVAC

HVAC websites have to handle two very different visitors. The repair visitor has a system that stopped working and wants to know if you can come today. The replacement visitor is planning a big purchase. They're comparing furnaces and heat pumps, wondering whether to go ductless, asking about rebates and financing and looking at two or three companies. That decision can take weeks, and your website is doing the selling while you're not in the room.

Most HVAC sites serve neither one well. The homepage has a stock photo of a condenser and a generic "Heating & Cooling Services" headline. All the system types are crammed onto one page. There's no explanation of the replacement process, no clear financing page, and the maintenance plan is a single paragraph. On top of that, the service area is one line of town names, which does almost nothing in search.

What that costs you:

- Repair visitors who can't find a phone number fast enough on mobile
- Replacement shoppers who leave to research elsewhere and find a competitor's site
- No ranking for system-specific searches like "ductless mini split installer" in your towns
- A maintenance plan nobody understands well enough to buy online

## What we build for HVAC companies

- **Two clear paths from the homepage.** "Need a repair?" with click-to-call and booking, and "Planning a replacement?" leading to system pages and a consultation request.
- **Pages for each system you work on.** Furnaces, boilers, central air, heat pumps, ductless mini-splits, and indoor air quality if you offer it. Each covers repair, replacement, how to tell when it's time and what affects cost.
- **A replacement process page** that walks through the consultation, sizing, proposal, install day and follow-up, so buyers know what to expect.
- **Financing and rebate pages** that explain your options plainly and link to the relevant programs, without promising specific amounts that may change.
- **A maintenance plan page** with what's included and an online sign-up or booking form.
- **Town pages for your service area**, each written around the local housing stock, whether that's older homes on oil heat or newer construction with heat pumps.
- **Fast load times, clean structure and CRM hookups**, so every call and form gets tracked.

## What to look for (even if you don't hire us)

Check your site, or any designer's proposal, for these:

- Can a repair visitor call you in one tap from the top of the page on mobile?
- Does each system type have its own page?
- Is there a page explaining your replacement process and financing?
- Can someone understand and sign up for your maintenance plan online?
- Do you have town pages, and are they genuinely different from each other?
- Are the photos from your own installs?
- Will the designer write the copy, and will forms flow into your CRM?
