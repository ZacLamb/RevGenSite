Facebook Ads for Remodelers: Offers, Creative and Lead Quality

October 18, 2026 · Digital Marketing

Meta ads (Facebook and Instagram) work differently from Google for a remodeler. Nobody is searching for you. You're interrupting someone scrolling past their cousin's vacation photos, and you have about a second to make them stop on a picture of a kitchen.

That's a real advantage for visual work like kitchens, baths, basements, decks and built-ins. It also means the leads tend to be earlier and softer than a search lead. Here's how to run Meta ads that bring in projects you actually want.

Start with the offer, not the ad

"Quality kitchen remodeling, call today" is not an offer. It's a description. A good Meta offer gives someone a reason to raise their hand now, even if the project is six months out.

Offers that tend to fit remodeling:

  • A free design consultation with a clear deliverable, like a layout sketch or a rough budget range.
  • A project cost guide for your area: "What a bathroom remodel costs on the South Shore," delivered by text or email.
  • Seasonal booking windows: "We're scheduling spring deck builds now. Six openings left." Only say this if it's true.
  • A specific project type: "Basement finishing for homes built before 1980" speaks to one homeowner very clearly.

Avoid deep discounts. They attract price shoppers and train people to wait for the next sale. If you mention financing terms, be aware that ads promoting credit can fall under Meta's Financial Products and Services category, which has its own rules.

Creative: use your real projects

Stock photos of glossy kitchens look like every other ad. Your own work, even shot on a phone, is more believable.

What tends to stop the scroll:

  • Before-and-after pairs, same angle, side by side or as a two-image carousel.
  • Short walk-through videos of a finished room, 15 to 30 seconds, shot vertically for Reels and Stories.
  • Process clips: framing going up, tile being set, a reveal when the plastic comes down.
  • The owner on camera for 20 seconds explaining one thing homeowners get wrong about planning a remodel.

Write the copy like a person, not a brochure:

"This Weymouth kitchen had a wall in the wrong place and about four feet of usable counter. We moved the wall, added an island and kept the original window. If your kitchen works against you, send us a photo and we'll tell you what's possible."

Put the town and the problem in the copy. Homeowners respond to houses that look like theirs.

Lead forms vs. landing pages

Meta's instant forms let people submit without leaving the app, pre-filled with their name, email and phone. That convenience is why they generate more leads, and why some of those leads barely remember filling them out.

Instant lead form Landing page
Volume Higher Lower
Cost per lead Usually lower Usually higher
Lead intent Mixed Stronger
Room to show your work Limited Full gallery, reviews, process
Tracking setup Simpler Needs Meta pixel and conversion events

Two settings that help with instant forms:

  1. Choose the "higher intent" form type, which adds a review screen before submitting.
  2. Add qualifying questions (more below), which slows down the people who aren't serious.

If you use a landing page, make it specific to the ad: same project type, same photos, a short form, a phone number and reviews. Don't send a kitchen ad to your homepage.

Qualifying questions that save your estimator's time

Every unqualified lead costs a call, maybe a site visit, maybe an afternoon. Ask a few multiple-choice questions on the form:

  • What project are you planning? Kitchen / bathroom / basement / deck / addition / other
  • When are you hoping to start? Within 3 months / 3 to 6 months / 6 to 12 months / just researching
  • Do you own the home? Yes / No
  • Rough budget range? Use ranges that match your real minimums.
  • Town or ZIP code. To screen out jobs outside your service area.

Keep it to three to five questions. Use the answers to route leads: "within 3 months" goes to an immediate call; "just researching" goes to a nurture sequence with project photos and your cost guide.

About Meta's housing special ad category

As of 2026, Meta requires advertisers to declare a special ad category for ads about housing, employment, financial products and services, and social issues, elections or politics. Meta's housing category covers housing opportunities and related services, and its definition includes items like homeowners insurance, mortgage loans and housing repairs. Whether a particular remodeling ad must use it depends on what the ad promotes, so read Meta's current definitions before you launch.

If an ad does run under the housing category, expect limits: age and gender can't be narrowed, ZIP code targeting isn't available, location targeting has a minimum radius, lookalike audiences aren't available and some detailed targeting is removed. Plan your creative and copy to qualify people, since targeting can't do as much of that work.

Follow-up speed decides whether Meta works

A Meta lead who filled out a form while watching TV will forget they did it within the hour. If you call them the next day, you'll hear "I don't remember that."

  • Send leads straight into your CRM, not an email inbox or a spreadsheet you download weekly.
  • Text within a minute or two: "Hi Sarah, this is Mike from [Company]. Got your request about the kitchen. Is now a good time for a quick call, or should I text you a couple of times to pick from?"
  • Call within the first few minutes during business hours.
  • Follow up for at least a week. Many people respond on the third or fourth touch.

We cover the why behind this in speed to lead for home services, and what to do after you've sent a price in estimate follow-up sequences for contractors.

Measuring what matters

Meta's dashboard shows cost per lead. That number alone will mislead you. Track each lead through to:

  1. Contacted
  2. Consultation or estimate held
  3. Proposal sent
  4. Signed project and its value

Then calculate cost per signed project by campaign. A campaign with cheap leads that never sign is worse than one with expensive leads that do. FAQ

Common questions

Do Facebook ads work for remodeling contractors?

They can, especially for visual projects like kitchens, baths, decks and basements, because people respond to real before-and-after photos in their feed. The catch is that Meta leads are earlier in the buying process than search leads, so qualifying and fast follow-up matter more.

Should remodelers use Facebook lead forms or a landing page?

Lead forms usually get more leads at a lower cost, and a landing page usually gets fewer but more serious ones. Many remodelers start with a lead form that uses a few qualifying questions, then test a landing page once they know their numbers.

Do remodeling ads have to use Meta's housing special ad category?

Not automatically. As of 2026, Meta's housing category covers housing opportunities and related services, and its definition includes housing repairs, so some remodeling ads may need it. Review Meta's current definitions for your ad, because the category limits your targeting options.
