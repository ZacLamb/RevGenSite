Google Business Profile for Medical Practices: Setup Guide

Photo: Direct Media · CC0
November 20, 2026 · Digital Marketing

When someone searches "dentist near me" or "physical therapist open Saturday," the Google listing is often the first thing they see, and sometimes the only thing. For a medical or wellness practice, a few settings do most of the work: the categories, the services list, the appointment link and how you handle reviews. Details below reflect Google's tools as of November 2026, so check the settings in your own dashboard.

Decide which profiles you need

Start with one profile for each real location. Google's guidelines also let individual practitioners have their own profiles in some cases. For a location with several eligible public-facing practitioners, Google says the organization should have its own profile separate from the practitioner's. When several practitioners share a location, a practitioner's profile title should be only the person's name, without the organization name.

Three rules keep you out of trouble:

  • Google advises against multiple profiles for the same individual to cover separate specializations.
  • A practice name stuffed with specialties, localities or superlatives is a common reason clinic profiles get suspended. Use your real, signed name.
  • When a provider leaves, remove or transfer their profile instead of leaving a stale listing that points patients at someone who is gone.

Sources interpret the practitioner rules differently, especially for larger groups. Read Google's current guidelines before creating anyone's profile, because a duplicate listing can be treated as a violation and suspended.

Pick categories that describe what you are

Google Business Profile supports one primary category and up to nine additional categories, but you don't need to use every slot. The primary one matters most. It tells Google what your business is, and it decides which features and attributes you get. That includes whether the appointment link field is available for your profile.

The test Google's guidelines suggest is "this business IS a" rather than "this business HAS a." The goal is to describe the business as a whole, not to list every service, product or amenity.

Think before you change your primary category later. Expect a possible re-verification when you change categories. Your website should also match. If your primary category is Chiropractor, your homepage and service pages should say so plainly, and each major service you list should have its own page.

Fill in the services list

Categories say what you are. Services say what you do. In the editor, choose Edit services, then Add more services, pick from the suggestions, or add a custom service if yours isn't listed. The suggestions are tailored to your business type.

For a medical or wellness practice, a good services list looks like this:

  1. List the services patients actually search for, such as "teeth whitening," "dry needling" or "annual physical."
  2. Write one or two plain sentences for each. Say who it's for and what the visit involves.
  3. Group related services so the list reads cleanly.
  4. Leave prices blank when cost depends on insurance or the exam. Pricing is optional, and you can list a fixed price, a range, or nothing.
  5. Remove anything you no longer offer.

Google says to list all your services on one profile, not create separate profiles for each service. Individual services also belong in the services list, not in extra categories.

Avoid clinical claims you can't back up, such as "cures" or "guaranteed relief." Have whoever handles compliance for your practice review the wording.

Add appointment links that work

The booking link is where a listing view turns into a scheduled visit. Google lets you add up to 10 links per category, and the booking type helps customers book an appointment. See Google's help page on local business links for the current steps. There are two routes:

  • Your own link. You add your own booking link to your Business Profile. The button opens your booking page, wherever it is hosted. Your profile must be verified before you can add links.
  • A scheduling partner. A native Book button from a Reserve with Google partner walks the patient through Google's own flow, while a custom link hands them off to your scheduling page. The button only appears if your booking software is an approved partner.

Some practical checks:

  • Send patients to a page where they can pick a time. Google's policies say booking links must go to a page for your business where customers can complete the booking.
  • Don't send first-time visitors to a long intake form. Book first, then send forms. Our post on online patient intake forms covers that order.
  • Test the link on a phone, logged out of everything.
  • Look for links you didn't add. Search Engine Roundtable reported on November 9, 2026 that Google may be automatically adding "Book online" links to some local business profiles. Some of those links returned errors.
  • Know the limits on third-party links. You can't manually delete third-party booking providers from your profile settings. Providers are required to process opt-out requests within 5 business days.
  • Remember you won't see much data. Google notes that performance data isn't available for custom links. Use a tracking setup like the one in how to track where your leads come from.

Once booking works, cut no-shows with the reminder steps in reduce no-shows with appointment reminders. If you plan to text patients, read HIPAA and text messaging for small practices first.

Verification: show the office, not the patients

For healthcare, Google now leans heavily on video verification, where you record a short clip proving the office, signage and that you have access. You can't pick the method. Google's help page says methods are determined automatically and can't be changed.

Prepare before you hit record:

  • Record when the waiting room is empty or the office is closed to patients.
  • Never film patients, other people's faces, charts, screens, IDs, forms, mail or sensitive documents.
  • The video must be continuous and recorded through the Business Profile on a mobile device. Don't record it separately and upload it later.
  • Start outside with your signage, then the entrance, the front desk, and a treatment room with nothing identifying on display.

Reviews: ask the right way and reply carefully

The review rules matter more for practices, because two sets of rules apply at once.

Google's rules. Google doesn't allow offering incentives, such as payment, discounts or free goods or services, in exchange for a review. That covers the "free whitening kit for a five-star review" idea. Google also doesn't allow merchants to discourage negative reviews or selectively solicit positive ones. Send your review request to everyone you treat. Our guide to Google reviews for medical practices covers the full process.

HIPAA. HIPAA doesn't ban replies, but it limits what a reply can say. Even if a reviewer names themselves and describes their visit, you still can't confirm they're a patient without written authorization. The enforcement cases are real:

  • A Texas dental practice that answered Yelp reviews with patient names and health information paid a $10,000 HIPAA settlement and agreed to a corrective action plan.
  • A psychiatry practice was found to have impermissibly disclosed the PHI of four patients in response to negative Google reviews. It agreed to pay $30,000 and entered a two-year corrective action plan.

A safe reply states your standards in general terms, thanks the person, and invites them to call the office. It never mentions dates, treatments, insurance or whether they were seen. Limit who has access to respond to reviews. Also get written authorization before quoting a patient's review in your own marketing.

A 15-minute audit for your listing

  1. Search your practice name and confirm the name, address, phone and hours are correct.
  2. Check that the primary category is the most specific one that fits.
  3. Remove any category that is really a service.
  4. Compare your services list with your website's service pages.
  5. Click the appointment link on your phone and complete a test booking.
  6. Look for booking links you didn't add.
  7. Read your last ten reviews and your replies for anything that confirms a patient relationship.
  8. Make sure no former provider still has a profile at your address.

FAQ

Common questions

How many categories can a medical practice have on Google Business Profile?

One primary category and up to nine additional ones, though you don't need to fill them all. Choose the most specific primary category that describes what your practice is, and add others only for things you really offer.

Can I reply to patient reviews on Google?

Yes, but keep the reply generic. Don't confirm the reviewer was a patient or mention any details of their care, even if they shared those details themselves.

Can patients book an appointment straight from my Google listing?

Yes. You can add your own appointment link, or connect a scheduling provider that supports Google's Book button. Check that the link opens a working page where the patient can finish booking.
