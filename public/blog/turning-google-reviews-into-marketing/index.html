How to Turn Google Reviews Into Your Best Marketing Asset

Updated October 18, 2026 · Digital Marketing

Reviews help your local rankings, but their bigger job is convincing a stranger to trust you with their house before you've spoken to them. Most service businesses have a handful of great reviews sitting on Google doing nothing else. This post covers how to get more of them within the rules, how to reply, and how to put them to work everywhere a customer is deciding.

Know the rules first

The rules on reviews changed in recent years, and they now come from two directions.

Google's policy. Google's content policy prohibits businesses from offering incentives such as payment, discounts or free goods or services for reviews, from selectively soliciting positive reviews, and from discouraging or prohibiting negative reviews (Google Maps user-contributed content policy). Google's help page for business owners also says reviews must reflect a genuine experience (Google Business Profile Help).

The FTC rule. In August 2024 the Federal Trade Commission announced a final rule that bans fake reviews and testimonials, bans incentives conditioned on a review expressing a particular sentiment, and bans certain review suppression tactics such as intimidation or baseless legal threats to remove negative reviews (FTC press release).

In plain terms for a contractor:

  • Ask every customer, the same way.
  • Don't offer anything in exchange for a review.
  • Don't screen customers with a "were you happy?" question and only send the Google link to the ones who say yes. That's review gating.
  • Never write reviews for yourself, have employees or family post them, or buy them.
  • Don't pressure anyone to change or remove a negative review.

Build a request system, not a habit

The businesses that collect the most reviews don't have pushier owners. They have a system that asks every time, at the right moment.

  1. Trigger on job completion. When a job is marked complete in your CRM, the request goes out. Not on a calendar date, and not when someone remembers.
  2. Send it while the work is fresh. Same day or the next morning. A painter's customer is happiest when the drop cloths are gone and the room looks finished.
  3. Use a direct link. Google lets you create a link or QR code that opens your review form. Send that link, not "find us on Google."
  4. Make it personal and short. Name the tech and the job.
  5. Follow up once. If there's no review after a few days, one polite reminder. Then stop.
  6. Stop the sequence when they review. Nobody wants a reminder after they've already done it.

Example text:

"Hi Karen, it's Dave from Coastal Painting. Thanks for having us out for the living room and stairwell. If you have a minute, a quick Google review helps other homeowners find us: [link]. Either way, we appreciate the work."

If you also leave a card or leave-behind, add a QR code to the same link. Before turning on automated texts, make sure your texting number is registered for A2P 10DLC. See our A2P 10DLC guide.

Reply to every review

Replies are public, and future customers read them.

Positive reviews. Thank them by name, mention the specific job, and keep it short. "Thanks, Mark. Glad the gutters held up through that storm last week." Mentioning the service and town where it fits naturally is fine. Keyword-stuffing your replies is not.

Negative reviews. One bad review won't sink you. An angry or missing reply can. A simple structure:

  1. Thank them and acknowledge the specific issue.
  2. Don't argue facts in public or share private job details.
  3. Say what you did or will do.
  4. Move it offline with a name and a direct number.

"Hi Linda, I'm sorry the trim touch-ups weren't done before we left. That's on us. I'd like to get a crew back out this week to finish it. Please call me directly at [number]. Steve, owner."

If a review is fake or breaks Google's policies, you can report it through your Business Profile. Don't threaten the reviewer.

Where reviews should show up besides Google

Most businesses stop at Google. Reviews do more work when they show up where people make decisions.

Where How to use reviews
Service pages Put reviews about that service on that page. An exterior painting page shows exterior painting reviews.
Town pages Feature reviews from customers in or near that town.
Ad landing pages Put two or three short, specific reviews near the form or call button.
Estimates and proposals Include a few reviews from similar jobs.
Meta ads Pair a review quote with a photo of the same job, if you have the customer's permission to show their home.
Follow-up texts Link to your reviews in estimate follow-ups so a hesitant customer sees what others said.
Your Google Business Profile posts Turn a great review into a post with a photo of the finished job.

Specific reviews beat generic ones. "They power-washed our deck and siding in one afternoon and cleaned up everything" sells more than "Great service!" When you choose reviews to feature, pick the ones that describe the work.

Reviews and your other marketing

Reviews feed into several other channels:

  • Local rankings. Google says review count and ratings are part of how it judges a business's prominence in local results (Google Business Profile Help).
  • Local Services Ads. Reviews show directly on these ads, which makes them part of your ad creative.
  • Repeat business. Past customers who left reviews are good candidates for a reactivation text campaign when it's time for the next room, the next season or the next cleaning.

Trade examples

  • Painting. Ask after the final walkthrough. Feature interior and exterior reviews on separate pages.
  • Gutter cleaning and pressure washing. Jobs are quick and frequent, so volume is your advantage. Ask after every visit, and use recent reviews in your spring and fall re-booking messages.
  • Remodeling. Projects are long, so ask at the final walkthrough, and use reviews that mention communication and cleanup, because that's what homeowners worry about.

FAQ

Common questions

Can I offer a discount for leaving a Google review?

No. Google's policy prohibits offering incentives such as payment, discounts or free services for reviews. The FTC's 2024 rule on reviews also bans incentives conditioned on a review being positive or negative.

Can I only send review requests to happy customers?

No. Google's policy prohibits selectively soliciting positive reviews, a practice often called review gating. Send the same request to every customer after the job.

Can I use my Google reviews on my website?

Quoting reviews accurately on your site is common practice. Use the reviewer's name as it appears publicly or a first name and town, don't edit the meaning, and link to your Google profile so visitors can see the source.
