Spring Cleanup Landscaping Leads: Handle the Call Surge

November 22, 2026 · AI & Automations

Spring cleanup is the first big wave of the year, and it doesn't arrive politely. One warm weekend can bring in more calls and web forms than your office handled all of February, and the crew is out on a mower or a rake when they land.

The landscapers who win this season aren't always the cheapest or the best known. They're the ones who answer first, quote clearly, and put the customer on a route before the next company calls back.

Why spring cleanup calls arrive in a pile

Cleanups are a seasonal, repeatable, low-ticket job, and homeowners treat them that way. They see leaves and matted-down beds when the snow goes, they search "spring cleanup near me," and they message two or three companies at once. The first one to reply with a clear next step usually gets the job.

That makes three things go wrong at the same time:

  • Calls stack up while crews are in the field. Nobody is at a desk to pick up.
  • Quotes slow down. If every cleanup needs a drive-by, your estimator becomes the bottleneck within a week.
  • Scheduling turns into guesswork. Without a clear capacity number per day, you either overbook and run late or turn people away out of caution.

The cost of one missed call is covered in more detail in the real cost of a missed call. Spring just compresses it into a few weeks.

What the surge costs you (a hypothetical)

Say your average cleanup is $450. Over one busy weekend, 20 callers reach voicemail. If even half of them would have booked, that's 10 jobs, or $4,500, gone from one weekend. Those homeowners didn't dislike you. They called the next name on the list.

You don't need to answer every call live. You need every caller to get a reply within minutes and a path to book. For more on why speed matters, see speed to lead for home services.

Step 1: Get your phone and listings ready before March

Do this in winter, when you have time.

  1. Check your Google Business Profile. Confirm your service areas, categories, and phone number. Use special hours for holidays or short-term schedule changes. Google's help page says special hours are for holidays or other temporary periods, and they only show when your hours are set to "Open with main hours" per Google's special hours guide. The rest of the profile is covered in our Google Business Profile checklist.
  2. Set call forwarding rules. Decide who gets calls when the office is busy. A ring group of two or three people beats one voicemail box.
  3. Record a better voicemail. Tell callers you'll text them within a few minutes, and give them a link to your cleanup request form.
  4. Make one page for cleanups. One URL with what's included, the towns you serve, and a request form. Send every ad, post, and listing there.

Step 2: Add a missed-call text-back and a quick intake form

A missed-call text-back sends an automatic text within seconds of a call you couldn't pick up. For spring cleanup, keep it short:

Hi, this is [Company]. Sorry we missed you. Are you looking for spring cleanup? Tap here to tell us about your property and we'll get you a price today: [link]

The link goes to a short form. Keep it to what you need to price the job:

  • Address and town
  • Lot size (small, medium, large, or acres)
  • Number of beds and whether they need edging or fresh mulch
  • Leaf, branch, or storm debris level
  • Two or three photos
  • Preferred days
  • Whether they're a past customer

Full setup steps are in our missed-call text-back guide.

A note on texting rules

Automatic texts come with two separate requirements. First, carriers require registration. As of 2026, Twilio's help center says you need A2P 10DLC registration if you send SMS or MMS to US numbers using long code numbers. Without it, messages can get filtered or blocked, so register in winter, not the week the calls start. Our A2P 10DLC walkthrough covers the steps.

Second, registration doesn't replace consent. One 2026 guide points out that TCPA consent rules apply regardless of registration. Ask your own lawyer how that applies to your lists, especially if you plan to text past customers.

Step 3: Quote cleanups by tier, not by site visit

Most cleanups can be priced from a form and photos. Build three or four tiers from your own numbers, such as small lot, standard lot, large lot, and "needs a visit." The price for each tier should come from your crew hours, dump fees, and what you've actually charged before.

Then have the system do the routine part:

  1. The form comes in.
  2. A tier is suggested based on the answers.
  3. A staff member checks the photos and confirms the price.
  4. The customer gets a text with the price and a choice of dates.

Only odd jobs go to an estimator: storm damage, steep slopes, overgrown lots, or anything with brush removal over what a crew can handle in a day. If your quoting needs more structure, the same logic works for a website quote tool.

Step 4: Control capacity so you don't overbook

Cleanup season is a capacity problem. Decide in advance:

  • Jobs per crew per day. Use your real numbers from last year, not your best day.
  • Which towns go on which days. Group by area so crews aren't driving across the county. This ties into lawn care route scheduling.
  • A waitlist rule. When the next two weeks are full, new requests get a message with the earliest date and the option to join the waitlist.

A waitlist keeps a "sorry, we're booked" from turning into a lost customer. When a cancellation happens, the system offers the opening to the next person in line.

Step 5: Follow up on quotes automatically

Many cleanup leads go quiet after the quote. They're comparing, not rejecting you. A short sequence recovers more of them than an owner remembering to call.

When Message
Right after the quote Price, dates available, and one tap to accept
Next day A short check-in asking if they have questions
Day 3 Remaining dates for their area
Day 6 Last friendly nudge; mention you're filling up

Stop the sequence the moment they book or reply. Our guide to an estimate follow-up sequence for contractors has fuller templates.

Pre-season checklist

Use this before the first warm weekend:

  • Google Business Profile reviewed and hours accurate
  • One cleanup page with a request form
  • A2P 10DLC registration complete (if texting from software)
  • Missed-call text-back tested from a real phone
  • Price tiers written down
  • Capacity numbers per crew and per day
  • Waitlist message ready
  • Quote follow-up messages written
  • Past customers contacted with a pre-booking offer (see the seasonal marketing calendar)

FAQ

Common questions

When should a landscaper start taking spring cleanup bookings?

Start before the snow melts. Open a pre-season list in winter, then confirm dates as soon as the ground and weather allow. Customers who already have a slot don't shop around when the first warm weekend hits.

Do I need to register to send automatic texts to spring cleanup leads?

As of 2026, if software sends texts from a standard 10-digit local number to US mobile phones, you need A2P 10DLC registration or carriers can filter the messages. Registration is separate from customer consent, so you still need permission to text people.

How do I quote spring cleanups without visiting every property?

Use a short intake form that asks for lot size, number of beds, leaf and debris load, and a few photos. Price from your own tiers, then visit only the odd properties, such as heavy storm damage or large lots.
