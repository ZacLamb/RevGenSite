CRM for Landscaping Companies: Contracts, Routes & Renewals

A landscaping company is really two businesses: recurring maintenance and project work. A CRM that treats them differently keeps contracts renewing and design leads moving.

The problem in landscaping

Most landscaping companies run two very different sales motions under one name. Weekly maintenance is a volume game: lots of accounts, modest tickets, priced by the visit or the season, sold mostly in late winter. Design/build is the opposite: fewer, bigger projects (a planting plan, a new lawn install, a patio with plantings) with a consultation, a design and a longer decision.

When both kinds of leads land in the same inbox, the design inquiries suffer. A homeowner asking about a full front-yard redesign gets the same quick "we'll swing by" reply as someone wanting a mow quote, and they go with the company that scheduled a proper consultation.

Then there is the calendar. In New England the year runs through spring cleanups, mowing season, summer installs, fall cleanups and leaf removal, then snow for anyone who plows. Each phase has its own rush of calls, and each is a chance to sell the next phase. Without a system, the spring cleanup customer never hears about a mowing contract, the mowing client is never offered aeration and overseeding, and last year's maintenance list gets re-signed by memory and whoever happens to call back.

What we build for landscaping companies

  • Two pipelines, not one. A maintenance pipeline (inquiry, property visit, proposal, contract signed, active) and a design/build pipeline (inquiry, consultation, design, proposal, deposit, install, complete). Different follow-up speed and tone for each. Our post on CRM pipeline stages shows how to choose stages that match your work.
  • Seasonal renewal automations. Every active maintenance customer gets a renewal offer at the time you choose, with reminders to anyone who hasn't signed and a call list of holdouts before your route is finalized.
  • Cleanup-to-contract conversion. After a spring cleanup is marked complete, the CRM sends a follow-up offering weekly maintenance for the season.
  • Upsell sequences by season. Mulch and edging in spring, aeration and overseeding in late summer, fall cleanup bookings, and snow contracts in the fall, sent to the customers each one fits.
  • Consultation booking. An online calendar for design consultations with reminders, so a design lead gets a real appointment in the first reply.
  • A team inbox that collects calls, texts, website forms and Facebook messages in one place for the office.
  • Monthly management to keep the customer list clean and adjust automations as your service mix changes.

How it works

  1. Map your process. We document how a maintenance account and a design project each move from first contact to signed, including how you handle renewals today.
  2. Build the pipelines. We set up the stages, forms, consultation calendar and seasonal automations to match.
  3. Migrate and train. We import last season's customer list and open proposals, then train the office and crew leads on the screens they will use.
  4. Manage month to month. We keep it clean through each season and adjust timing as you learn what works.

What to look for (even if you don't hire us)

  • Can it keep maintenance and design/build leads in separate pipelines with separate follow-up?
  • Can each customer record store the property details: lot size, service level, gate codes, notes for the crew?
  • Can it send renewal offers in bulk, then show you who hasn't responded?
  • Does it support texting from a registered number? As of 2026, US business texting generally requires A2P 10DLC registration.
  • Will it connect to the scheduling or invoicing tool your crews already use, or replace it cleanly?
  • Is someone available to adjust it when the season changes, or are you on your own?

FAQ

Common questions

Can a CRM handle landscape maintenance renewals?

Yes. Each maintenance customer can carry a contract end date or season, and the CRM can send renewal offers on a schedule each winter, flag anyone who hasn't signed, and create a task for a personal call on the larger accounts.

Should snow removal live in the same CRM as landscaping?

Usually yes, as its own pipeline. Many of your snow customers are your summer customers, so keeping them in one system lets you offer snow contracts to lawn clients in the fall and lawn contracts to snow clients in late winter.

Is a CRM the same as route scheduling software?

Not exactly. A CRM handles leads, sales, contracts and customer communication. Some landscapers run route and crew scheduling in a separate field service tool and connect it to the CRM; others keep it simple. We help you decide which fits your size.
