Digital Marketing for Plumbers: Ads & Local SEO That Book Jobs

Plumbing ad money disappears fast when an expensive click turns into a caller asking how to snake their own drain. Here's how to point the budget at the jobs you actually want.

The problem in plumbing

Plumbing marketing has a quirk most agencies miss: the same word can mean a quick service call or a job that ties up a crew for days. "Plumber" might be a dripping faucet or a collapsed sewer line. If your campaigns lump everything together, you end up paying the same per click for both and can't tell which one paid off.

Then there's timing. Emergency demand spikes in cold snaps when pipes freeze, during heavy rain when sump pumps fail, and on holidays when the whole family is using one bathroom. Planned work like water heater replacements and fixture upgrades follows a slower, steadier curve. A budget that runs flat all year misses both patterns.

And most plumbers can't answer the basic question: which of last month's jobs came from ads? The agency report says clicks went up. The office says the phone was busy. Nobody connects the two.

Other common leaks:

  • Ads running in towns you don't want to drive to
  • Search terms like "plumbing apprentice jobs" or "how to fix toilet" eating the budget
  • A Google Business Profile with old hours, few photos and reviews nobody replies to
  • Every ad pointing to the homepage instead of a page about the actual problem

What we build for plumbing companies

  • Separate emergency and planned-work campaigns. Emergency campaigns lean on call ads and are scheduled around when you can actually answer. Planned-work campaigns target water heater, repipe, sewer and fixture searches and send clicks to matching service pages.
  • Local Services Ads setup and management. Profile setup, service categories, budget and a regular review of the leads coming in so the ones that don't fit get flagged. Our comparison of Google Local Services Ads vs. Google Ads explains how the two fit together.
  • Geographic targeting by town. Ads only run where your trucks go, with the option to bid harder in the towns where your best jobs come from.
  • Negative keyword maintenance. Job seekers, DIY searches and parts shoppers get filtered out as they show up.
  • Google Business Profile work and review growth. Accurate hours, services, service area, photos of real jobs and a steady stream of fresh reviews.
  • Meta campaigns for planned work and past customers. Water heater replacement offers or seasonal reminders aimed at homeowners in your service area.
  • Monthly reporting tied to booked jobs by connecting campaigns to your CRM, so you see cost per booked job by campaign, not just cost per click.

How it works

  1. Audit. We review your ad accounts, Google Business Profile and website to find where money is leaking now.
  2. Build campaigns. We rebuild around your service area and the job types with the best margins.
  3. Launch and track. Every campaign is connected to your CRM so calls and forms are tied to a source.
  4. Optimize monthly. We report on what booked, cut what didn't and shift budget toward what's working.

What to look for (even if you don't hire us)

Ask any agency, or check your own accounts, for these:

  • Do you own the ad accounts? They should be in your business's name, with the agency added as a user.
  • Can they show the search terms report? If you see "plumber salary" in there, money is leaking.
  • Are calls tracked and recorded? Most plumbing leads are phone calls, and form-only tracking misses them.
  • Is reporting tied to booked jobs? Ask for cost per booked job, split by emergency and planned work.
  • Does the ad schedule match your phone coverage?
  • What happens to your Google Business Profile? It often drives as many calls as paid ads. Our Google Business Profile checklist for contractors is a good place to start.

FAQ

Common questions

Should a plumber run Google Local Services Ads or regular Google Ads?

Often both, for different jobs. As of 2026, Local Services Ads charge per lead and show a Google verification badge, which suits emergency calls. Search ads give you more control over keywords and landing pages, which suits planned work like water heater replacement or repiping.

Is it worth running plumbing ads at night?

Only if someone answers. Late-night emergency searches can be your best leads, but if the phone goes to voicemail you're paying for a competitor's job. Many plumbers schedule ads around their on-call coverage or pair them with an answering service.

Do Facebook ads work for plumbers?

Not for emergencies, since nobody scrolls Facebook looking for a plumber while their basement floods. Meta works better for planned offers to people in your area, like water heater replacement, tankless upgrades or seasonal reminders to past customers.
