CRM for Plumbing Companies: Dispatch, Estimates & Follow-Up

Most plumbing shops run dispatch from a group text and estimates from memory. A CRM built around how plumbing work actually moves fixes both without adding office staff.

The problem in plumbing

Plumbing has two speeds running at once. One is the emergency board: a clogged main line, a leaking supply line under a kitchen sink, no hot water on a Monday morning. Those jobs go from phone call to truck in an hour, and the only record is often a text from the office to a tech saying "123 Elm, backed up basement, call her when close."

The other speed is planned work: a water heater replacement quote, a tankless conversion, a bathroom rough-in for a remodeler, a whole-house repipe on an older home with galvanized lines. Those take a site visit, a written estimate and a customer who wants to think it over. That's where the money sits, and that's what falls through when the office is buried in emergency calls.

When the system is a group text plus a notebook, you see the same problems:

  • Nobody can say how many estimates are open or how much they're worth
  • A customer calls back about a quote and the office can't find who wrote it or what it said
  • Techs show up without knowing the customer had the same drain cleared twice last year
  • Past customers with aging water heaters never hear from you until theirs fails and they call whoever ranks first

What we build for plumbing companies

  • A service-call board. Stages like New Call, Dispatched, On Site, Completed and Invoiced, with the on-call tech assigned automatically and a timestamp on every move so you can see response times.
  • An estimate pipeline for planned work. Separate stages for Site Visit Booked, Estimate Sent, Following Up, Won and Lost, with the job type (water heater, repipe, sewer line, fixture install) tagged so you can see which kinds you close and which you don't.
  • Follow-up automations on open estimates. Tasks and reminders for the office, plus texts to the customer at set intervals, all stopping when they reply or sign.
  • One team inbox. Calls, texts, web forms, Google Business Profile messages and Facebook messages in one place so the office isn't checking five apps.
  • Online booking for non-urgent work. Customers can book a drain cleaning or estimate visit into open slots, with reminders and easy rescheduling.
  • Customer history on every record. Past jobs, equipment installed, install dates and notes the next tech can read before knocking on the door.
  • Monthly cleanup and management if you want us to keep it running: fixing broken automations, merging duplicates and adjusting stages as your mix of work changes.

If you run several crews and want to see where things stall, our guide to CRM pipeline stages for service businesses goes deeper on how to name and measure them.

How it works

  1. Map your process. We sit with whoever answers the phone and walk through an emergency call and a planned job from first ring to paid invoice.
  2. Build the pipeline. We set up stages, forms, calendars and automations to match what we mapped.
  3. Migrate and train. We bring in your customers and open estimates, then train the office and techs on the screens they'll actually use.
  4. Manage month to month. Optional ongoing management keeps the system clean as you add techs or services.

What to look for (even if you don't hire us)

Whoever builds your CRM, check for these:

  • Separate tracks for emergency and planned work. One pipeline for everything turns into a mess within a month.
  • Job type on every record. Without it you can't answer "how many water heater quotes did we lose last quarter?"
  • A mobile view techs will actually use. If it takes more than a few taps to mark a job done, they won't.
  • Lead source captured automatically. You want to know whether a repipe came from Google, a referral or a past customer. See how to track where your leads come from.
  • Your data is exportable. You should be able to leave with your customer list and job history.
  • Someone owns it after launch. CRMs decay when nobody is responsible for them.

FAQ

Common questions

Should emergency plumbing calls and scheduled jobs live in the same pipeline?

Usually not. Emergency work needs a short board measured in hours (received, dispatched, on site, done), while a repipe or water heater replacement needs an estimate pipeline measured in days or weeks. Keeping them apart stops urgent calls from burying open quotes.

Can a CRM replace the group text my techs use for dispatch?

It can replace the job details part. Techs get a text or app notification with the address, the problem, the customer's notes and gate codes, and they update the job status from their phone. Your techs can still chat with each other, but the job record stops living in a thread.

How do I get my existing customer list into a new CRM?

We export from whatever you use now, whether that's a spreadsheet, QuickBooks customers or an old field service app, clean up duplicates and bad numbers, and tag customers by past job type so you can market to them later, for example everyone who had a water heater installed eight or more years ago.
