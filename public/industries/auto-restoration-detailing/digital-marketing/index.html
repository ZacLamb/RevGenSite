Marketing for Auto Detailing & Restoration Shops

Detailing customers search nearby and book this week. Restoration clients may drive hours and decide over months. One marketing plan has to handle both without wasting money on either.

The problem in restoration and detailing marketing

Shops in this space are often great at content and weak at turning it into booked work. The specific problems:

  • Two businesses, one budget. Detailing is local, frequent and price-compared. Restoration is high-trust, high-ticket and regional. Running them through the same campaign means paying regional click prices for a local wash, or local targeting that misses restoration clients two states away.
  • High-value services hide behind vague ads. "Premium auto detailing" doesn't compete with a shop advertising a specific coating package with real photos. Buyers of coatings and film are searching for those terms.
  • Great Instagram, no tracking. A build video can get wide reach and still produce zero inquiries you can trace. Without tracking, you can't tell if the content is paying for itself.
  • Reviews lag behind the work. Many detailing customers leave thrilled and never write a review, so the Google listing undersells the shop.
  • Long decision cycles. Someone researching a frame-off restoration or a full-car PPF install may visit your site several times over weeks. Without remarketing and follow-up, they forget which shop impressed them.

What we build for restoration and detailing shops

  1. Service-specific Google search campaigns. Separate campaigns for detailing, paint correction, ceramic coating and PPF, each with tight local targeting and its own landing page. Restoration gets its own campaign with broader geography.
  2. Meta campaigns built on your builds. Before-and-after reels, correction close-ups and restoration stage videos promoted to enthusiasts and owners in your area, using your real work.
  3. Remarketing for high-ticket services. People who viewed coating, PPF or restoration pages see your work again over the following weeks, so a long decision doesn't end with them forgetting you.
  4. Google Business Profile and reviews. Categories, services, photos of recent work and a steady review request after every completed detail or delivery.
  5. Show-season pushes. Short, focused campaigns around regional shows and the start of driving season, coordinated with your posting schedule.
  6. Booked-job reporting. Every lead tagged by source in the CRM, so monthly reports show booked details, coating jobs and restoration consults by channel.

How it works

  1. Audit. We review your ad accounts, Google listing, website and social profiles to find quick fixes and wasted spend.
  2. Build campaigns. We separate detailing from restoration and build campaigns around your highest-margin services.
  3. Launch and track. Each campaign connects to your CRM, so a lead is followed from first click to paid invoice.
  4. Optimize monthly. We cut what isn't booking work and shift budget toward what is.

No honest agency can promise a set number of bookings or a fixed return. What you should expect is clear tracking and a plan that changes based on what the numbers show.

What to look for (even if you don't hire us)

  • Are detailing and restoration in separate campaigns? If not, ask why.
  • Do ads point to service pages? A coating ad should land on a coating page with packages and photos.
  • Is the creative your work? Stock car photos are a bad sign in an industry where buyers can spot them instantly.
  • Are Instagram leads tracked? Ask how DMs and form fills from Meta get attributed in your reports.
  • Do reports show booked jobs? Followers and impressions alone don't tell you whether spend worked.
  • Do you own the accounts? Ad accounts, pixel and Google listing should all be in your name.

FAQ

Common questions

Is Instagram or Google better for a detailing shop?

They do different jobs. Google search catches people already looking for ceramic coating, PPF or a detail near them. Instagram and Facebook are where enthusiasts discover shops through transformations and build progress. Most shops benefit from Google for demand and Meta for reputation and reach.

How far should a restoration shop target its ads?

Usually much wider than a detailing campaign. People will travel for the right restoration shop, so targeting can cover a region, while detailing ads should stay close to the shop or your mobile service area.

Can I advertise around car shows?

Yes. Short campaigns aimed at the area around a show, plus posts about the cars you're bringing or the builds attending, can help people find you before and after the event. Treat it as a reputation play rather than expecting bookings from the weekend itself.
