AI & Automation for Gutter Cleaning and Pressure Washing

When the average job is a couple hundred dollars, you can't afford to spend twenty minutes on the phone winning it. Here's how to let automation handle the quoting, re-booking and rain-day shuffle.

The problem in gutter and pressure washing

This trade runs on volume. A gutter cleaning or driveway wash is a small ticket, so the business works only when inquiries convert fast, routes stay dense and last year's customers come back. Here's where time leaks out:

  • Quote requests come in while you're on a ladder. A homeowner asks "how much for my gutters?" at 11 a.m. You see it at 6 p.m., after they've booked whoever texted back first. On an impulse-driven service, the first credible reply usually wins.
  • Re-booking depends on memory. Spring and fall customers should come back, but few companies have a system that asks them at the right time.
  • Weather blows up the schedule. Two days of October rain can push thirty jobs. Someone has to text every one, offer new times and re-sequence routes, unpaid.
  • Reviews get skipped. Crews move to the next stop and the "how did we do?" text never goes out.

What we build for gutter and pressure washing companies

Automation here should be boring and dependable:

  1. Instant quote replies. A text and email agent that answers new inquiries within a minute, asks the questions that set price (stories, footage, guards, surface type) and returns a range or books an estimate.
  2. Missed-call text-back with AI follow-up. When you can't pick up, the caller gets a text right away. An AI chat agent trained on your services keeps the conversation going and hands off to you when it should.
  3. Seasonal re-booking sequences. Each customer gets a re-book offer timed off their last cleaning date: spring for last spring's customers, fall for fall, and a "both seasons" offer for people who booked twice.
  4. Rain-day rescheduling. Mark a date as weathered out, and every affected customer receives a reschedule text with open slots that keep your routes grouped by area.
  5. Review requests on job completion. When the crew marks a job done, the customer gets a short review request with a direct link, plus one reminder if they don't respond.

How it works

  1. Audit. We walk through how an inquiry becomes a scheduled job today and where your hours go: quoting, confirming, rescheduling, chasing reviews.
  2. Design. We write the pricing rules, questions and handoff points with you, so the agent quotes the way you would and knows when to stop.
  3. Build and connect. We wire the agents and sequences into your CRM, phone number and calendar so every conversation and booking lands in one place.
  4. Tune and support. We watch the first 60 days of real conversations, fix the answers that miss, and adjust as your seasons and pricing change.

What to look for (even if you don't hire us)

Before you buy any automation or AI tool for a gutter or washing business, check these:

  • Can it price from your rules? If the tool can only say "someone will get back to you," it isn't solving the speed problem.
  • Is texting registered? Business texting in the US goes through A2P 10DLC registration. Ask the vendor who handles it and what happens if registration is rejected.
  • Does re-booking key off the customer's own history? A blanket blast to your whole list is not the same thing.
  • Can you reschedule a whole day in a few clicks? Ask them to show you, not tell you.
  • Is there a clear handoff? The agent should alert you or book a callback the moment a question goes past what it knows.
  • Can you read every conversation? You should see exactly what the AI told each customer.

FAQ

Common questions

Can an AI agent actually quote a gutter cleaning over the phone?

It can give a range or a firm price if you give it clear rules, such as story count, footage bands and whether guards are installed. Anything outside those rules should trigger a callback instead of a guess.

How do I re-book last fall's customers without texting everyone at once?

Trigger the offer from each customer's last service date, not a calendar blast. Replies spread out over a few weeks, and you can fill routes neighborhood by neighborhood.

What happens to booked jobs when it rains?

A good setup lets you mark a day as weathered out, texts every affected customer a few new slots, and updates the calendar when they pick one.
