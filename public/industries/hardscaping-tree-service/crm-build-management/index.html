CRM for Tree Service & Hardscaping: Storm Work to Patio Projects

A storm cleanup and a $40,000 patio don't belong in the same pipeline. A CRM built for this trade keeps urgent tree work and long hardscape sales cycles on separate tracks in one system.

The problem in hardscaping and tree service

The same company might be cutting a pine off a garage roof at 7 a.m. and walking a backyard for a retaining wall and paver patio at 2 p.m. Those two jobs have nothing in common except the customer list. The tree call needs a response in minutes. The hardscape project needs a careful estimate, a design conversation and patient follow-up over weeks.

When both live in one notebook or one generic pipeline:

  • Emergency calls get lost in the noise. After a storm, the list of callers grows faster than anyone can sort it, and urgent jobs wait behind routine pruning requests.
  • Hardscape leads go quiet. Big projects stall in the proposal stage because the crew that should be following up is running chainsaws.
  • Equipment gets double-booked. Two jobs both need the bucket truck or the mini excavator on Thursday, and nobody notices until Wednesday night.
  • Estimates are scattered. Measurements, photos and notes from site visits end up on phones and clipboards, not on the customer record.
  • Seasonal swings aren't planned for. Spring hardscape demand and storm-driven tree work collide, and winter goes quiet without a plan to fill it.

What we build for hardscaping and tree companies

  1. An emergency tree pipeline. New Storm Call, Triaged Emergency, Crew Dispatched, Site Secured, Cleanup Scheduled, Complete and Invoiced, with fields for hazard type, structure damage and insurance claim status.
  2. A planned tree work pipeline. Request, Assessment Booked, Quote Sent, Scheduled, Complete, for pruning, removals and stump grinding that can be planned around weather and crew availability.
  3. A hardscape project pipeline. Inquiry, Site Visit, Design and Proposal, Follow-Up, Signed, Materials Ordered, Dig Safe and Permits, Scheduled, Installed and Paid, with the expected install season stored on each opportunity.
  4. On-site estimate records. A mobile-friendly form for measurements, slope, access width for machines, material selections and photos, saved straight to the customer record.
  5. Equipment and crew calendars. Cranes, bucket trucks, excavators and skid steers set up as resources alongside crews, so bookings respect what's actually available.
  6. Cross-sell automations. Tree removal customers tagged for a follow-up about stump grinding, regrading or a new patio, and hardscape customers tagged for seasonal tree care.

How it works

  1. Map your process. We document how an emergency call, a pruning request and a patio inquiry each move through your business today.
  2. Build the pipeline. We set up separate pipelines, forms, calendars and automations for each type of work.
  3. Migrate and train. We import existing customers and open estimates, then train office staff and crew leads on the screens they'll use.
  4. Manage month to month. Optional ongoing management keeps pipelines and equipment calendars current as your fleet and crews change.

What to look for (even if you don't hire us)

When you evaluate a CRM for this trade, check:

  • Can it run separate pipelines with different follow-up speeds for emergency, routine tree and hardscape work?
  • Can you book equipment as a resource, not just people?
  • Will estimators be able to enter measurements and photos from a phone on site?
  • Can you pull a list of everyone who called during a storm for follow-up once crews are free?
  • Can you track insurance claim status on storm jobs?
  • Will reports show revenue and close rate separately for tree and hardscape work?

FAQ

Common questions

Should tree service and hardscaping use separate CRMs?

No, they usually share customers. The homeowner who calls about a fallen oak may want a new patio where it stood. One CRM with separate pipelines, follow-up timing and reports for each side keeps the shared customer history while letting each pipeline move at its own pace.

Can a CRM help schedule equipment like bucket trucks, cranes and skid steers?

Yes. Equipment can be set up as bookable resources on a calendar, so a job that needs the crane or the excavator can't be booked when that machine is already committed. It doesn't replace a full fleet system, but it prevents the most common double bookings.

How should a CRM handle hardscape estimates that take weeks to decide?

Put each one in a proposal stage with a follow-up sequence and a task for the estimator to call at set intervals. Track the expected install season on the opportunity so spring projects get attention over the winter.
