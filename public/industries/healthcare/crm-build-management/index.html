CRM for Healthcare & Wellness Practices: Intake to Recall

Your EHR keeps the chart. A CRM handles everything before the first visit and between visits: the inquiry, the follow-up, the recall and the patient who quietly stopped coming.

The problem in healthcare and wellness practices

A practice can have a full book this week and still be losing patients it never sees leave. The pattern shows up across chiropractic, PT, dental, med spa and counseling practices:

  • New-patient inquiries get handled once, then lost. Someone calls or fills out a form, gets a voicemail back, and the trail ends. Without a pipeline, nobody knows how many inquiries became first visits.
  • Patients fall out of care plans. A patient misses a follow-up and simply doesn't rebook. Nobody notices until months later, if ever.
  • Recalls depend on a report someone has to run. Recall visits generate steady revenue and continuity of care, but only if someone actually pulls the list and makes the contact.
  • Messages are scattered. Calls, voicemail, web forms, texts, Facebook messages and emails come in through different tools. Staff can't tell what's been answered.
  • Tasks live on sticky notes. Callbacks, insurance follow-ups and records requests sit on paper.

What we build for healthcare and wellness practices

  1. A new-patient pipeline. Inquiry, Contacted, Scheduled, Intake Sent, Intake Complete, First Visit Attended, Returning. You can see where prospective patients stall and follow up before they book elsewhere.
  2. Care-continuity tracking. Fields for last visit and next scheduled visit, with a saved view of active patients who have no future appointment so staff can reach out.
  3. Recall and reactivation lists. Automated lists built on your recall intervals and inactivity windows, connected to minimal-content outreach your practice approves.
  4. Appointment booking and reminders. Calendars by provider and visit type, with confirmation, reminder and reschedule messages that avoid clinical detail.
  5. One team inbox. Calls, texts, web forms, email and social messages in a single shared inbox, assigned to staff, with a clear view of what's unanswered.
  6. Front-desk tasks. Callback requests, records requests and insurance follow-ups as assigned tasks with due dates.
  7. Ongoing management. Data cleanup, list hygiene, opt-out tracking and adjustments as your services and providers change.

A rule we follow on every healthcare build: clinical information stays in your EHR. The CRM holds the minimum needed to communicate and schedule.

How it works

  1. Map your process. We document how an inquiry becomes a patient today and where patients drop out, from first call to recall.
  2. Build the pipeline. We configure stages, fields, calendars and automations to match. Before patient information is loaded, your practice should confirm vendor agreements, such as a BAA, with your compliance advisor.
  3. Migrate and train. We bring in the contact data you decide belongs in the CRM, clean it up, and train front-desk staff on daily use.
  4. Manage month to month. If you choose ongoing management, we keep data clean and lists accurate.

RGS doesn't provide compliance advice or certify any platform as HIPAA compliant. GoHighLevel is our main platform, and we'll help you ask any vendor the right questions.

What to look for (even if you don't hire us)

  • Will the vendor sign a BAA on your plan? Get the answer in writing and have your compliance advisor review it.
  • Can you limit what staff see? User permissions should match job roles.
  • Does it keep a record of opt-outs and consent? Texting and email need clear opt-in and opt-out tracking.
  • Can it show patients with no future appointment? That single view drives most reactivation work.
  • Does it connect to your scheduling system? If appointments have to be entered twice, staff will stop doing it.
  • Can you export your data? You should be able to leave with your history.

FAQ

Common questions

What's the difference between a CRM and our EHR?

The EHR holds clinical records, charting and often billing. A CRM manages the relationship side: inquiries, scheduling follow-up, reminders, recalls, reactivation and reviews. Keeping clinical details in the EHR and only what's needed for communication in the CRM is the cleaner setup.

Can we use a CRM for patient communication under HIPAA?

Many practices do, but it depends on the vendor, the plan and how you configure and use it. Ask the CRM vendor whether it will sign a business associate agreement (BAA) for your plan, and have your compliance advisor review the setup before patient information goes in.

How do we find patients who dropped off a care plan?

Track the last visit date and whether a next appointment is booked. A saved list of patients with no future appointment and a last visit older than your chosen window becomes your reactivation list, which staff or an automated sequence can work through.
