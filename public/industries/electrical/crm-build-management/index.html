CRM for Electricians: Estimates, Permits & Job Tracking

An electrical job has more steps between yes and paid than most trades: estimate, deposit, permit, utility coordination, rough-in, inspection. A CRM should track all of them, not just the lead.

The problem in electrical

Electrical contractors lose more work to slow follow-up than to price. A homeowner fills out a form asking about a 200-amp service upgrade, a second person calls about an EV charger in the garage and a property manager emails about lighting retrofits in a strip of storefronts. If the shop owner is also the lead estimator, those requests sit until a free evening, and by then at least one of those customers has signed with someone else.

Electrical work also has a long tail after the yes. A panel upgrade often means a permit, coordinating a disconnect and reconnect with the utility, a rough or service inspection and a final. A generator install adds a gas line, a transfer switch and sometimes a pad. Every one of those is a chance for a job to stall quietly while the customer wonders what's going on.

What it looks like without a system:

  • Estimates written in a separate app with no reminder to follow up
  • No way to tell which of last quarter's leads became paid jobs
  • Residential service calls, commercial bids and after-hours emergencies all in one inbox
  • Permit status tracked in the owner's head or on a whiteboard in the shop
  • Change orders agreed to on site and never written down

What we build for electrical contractors

  • Three intake routes. Residential, commercial and emergency requests are tagged at intake (from the form, the call or the person answering) and sent to their own pipeline with their own response-time rules.
  • A residential estimate pipeline. Stages from Request Received through Site Visit, Estimate Sent, Follow-Up, Deposit Paid and Scheduled, with job type tagged: panel or service upgrade, EV charger, generator, rewiring, lighting, troubleshooting.
  • A commercial bid pipeline. Room for multiple contacts per job (owner, GC, property manager), bid due dates, and a note field for scope and walkthrough details.
  • Permit and inspection tracking. Fields and stages for permit status and inspection dates, with tasks that remind the office when an inspection needs booking.
  • Estimate follow-up automations. Reminders to the estimator plus texts and emails to the customer at set intervals after a quote goes out. Our estimate follow-up sequence guide lays out timing you can copy.
  • A team inbox and shared calendar so calls, texts and forms are visible to whoever is in the office, and site visits book into real availability.
  • Reporting from lead to paid invoice, including which lead sources produce signed panel upgrades versus small service calls.

How it works

  1. Map your process. We walk through a residential job and a commercial bid from first contact to final inspection and payment.
  2. Build the pipeline. We configure stages, forms, calendars and automations to match.
  3. Migrate and train. We import your customers and open quotes, then train the office and estimators.
  4. Manage month to month. If you want, we keep the system clean and adjust it as your mix of residential and commercial work shifts.

What to look for (even if you don't hire us)

Whatever platform you use, it should handle these:

  • Response timers on new requests. You should see at a glance which leads haven't been touched in 24 hours.
  • Job type and lead source on every record, so you can answer which services and channels make money.
  • Permit and inspection fields, or at least a stage that makes waiting jobs visible.
  • Multiple contacts per commercial job. GCs and property managers are rarely the same person who pays.
  • Simple mobile updates for the field. Electricians on a ladder won't fill out a long form.
  • A clear owner after launch. Who fixes it when an automation breaks?

FAQ

Common questions

Can a CRM track permits and inspections for electrical jobs?

Yes. We add stages or fields for permit applied, permit issued, rough inspection and final inspection, with reminders so nobody forgets to schedule the inspector. It won't file the permit for you, but it makes it obvious which jobs are waiting on one.

How should an electrician separate commercial and residential leads?

Use different pipelines. A homeowner asking about a panel upgrade decides in days and cares about price and scheduling. A property manager or GC asking for a tenant fit-out bid has a longer cycle, more contacts and often a bid deadline. Mixing them hides both.

What's the first thing to fix if estimate requests sit unanswered?

Make sure every request lands in one place and creates a task with a due time. Most delays aren't laziness. The request came in by email or a text to a tech's personal phone and nobody saw it.
