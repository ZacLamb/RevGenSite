CRM for HVAC Companies: Pipelines, Renewals & Follow-Up

An HVAC company is really three businesses sharing a phone line: emergency service, equipment replacement and maintenance agreements. Your CRM should keep them apart and connect them at the right moments.

The problem in HVAC

HVAC demand comes in waves. The first heat wave of summer and the first real cold snap of fall bring a flood of no-cool and no-heat calls, and in New England those calls can come on top of each other with oil, gas and heat pump customers all at once. Then spring and late fall go quiet, and the techs who were working ten-hour days are looking for something to do.

The companies that handle this well do three things: they answer the surge, they turn repair calls on old equipment into replacement quotes, and they keep the slow months full with maintenance visits. All three depend on records. Who has a fifteen-year-old furnace? Who got a replacement quote in August and never decided? Whose maintenance agreement expires next month?

Without a real CRM, that information lives in a field service app nobody queries, a spreadsheet of agreement customers and the memory of your best tech. Typical results:

  • Maintenance agreements that quietly lapse because nobody sent a renewal
  • Replacement quotes with no follow-up after the first call
  • Service calls and install leads in one list, so big-ticket opportunities get the same treatment as a filter change
  • No way to see which ads or sources bring installs versus tune-ups

What we build for HVAC companies

  • A service call pipeline for no-heat, no-cool and repair work, with stages from New Call through Dispatched, Diagnosed, Repaired or Replacement Recommended.
  • A replacement and install pipeline that a service call can feed into when the tech recommends new equipment. Stages for Comfort Consult Booked, Proposal Sent, Financing in Progress, Sold and Install Scheduled.
  • Equipment records on every customer: system type, fuel, make, model and install year, so you can pull a list of every customer with a system past a certain age.
  • Maintenance agreement tracking: plan type, start date, visits due and renewal date, with automated reminders to book spring and fall visits.
  • Lead source tags so you can see whether installs came from ads, referrals, agreement customers or tech recommendations.
  • Online booking, reminders and a team inbox so the office isn't juggling calls, texts and web forms during a heat wave.
  • Monthly management if you want us to keep the stages, automations and reports current.

How it works

  1. Map your process. We walk through a service call, a replacement sale and a maintenance agreement from start to renewal.
  2. Build the pipeline. We set up stages, equipment fields, forms, calendars and automations to fit.
  3. Migrate and train. We import customers, equipment data and agreement lists, then train the office, CSRs and comfort advisors.
  4. Manage month to month. Optional ongoing management keeps the system working through each season.

What to look for (even if you don't hire us)

Any HVAC CRM, whoever builds it, should let you:

  • Pull a list of customers by equipment age and fuel type. That list is your off-season replacement campaign.
  • See every active maintenance agreement and its renewal date.
  • Separate service and replacement pipelines, with a clear handoff when a tech recommends replacement.
  • Record why a replacement quote stalled, not just that it did.
  • Report on revenue by lead source and job type, not just lead counts.
  • Handle surge volume. Test it with a busy day's worth of calls before peak season, not during it.

FAQ

Common questions

What should an HVAC CRM track that a generic CRM doesn't?

Equipment details on every customer record (make, model, fuel type, install year), maintenance agreement status and renewal dates, and whether a lead is a service call or a replacement opportunity. Those fields drive most of the useful automations.

How should HVAC companies handle replacement quotes that don't close right away?

Put them in their own pipeline with follow-up tasks and automated touches, and record why they stalled, such as waiting on financing, getting other quotes or waiting for a rebate. Many come back months later when the old system fails again, so you want the history.

Can a CRM help us sell maintenance agreements?

It can make sure every service call and install customer gets offered one, track who has an active plan, and remind them before it lapses. The selling is still your techs and office, but the system makes sure nobody gets skipped.
