CRM for Remodelers: Track Every Project Stage, Not Just Leads

A kitchen remodel can take months from the first call to the final punch list. Most CRMs are built to close a lead and forget it, which is exactly where a remodeling business needs the system to keep going.

The problem in carpentry and remodeling

Remodeling sales cycles are long and expensive. A homeowner calls about a kitchen in March, meets you for a consult in April, waits on a design and a number through May, and maybe signs in June for a fall start. Over those months the lead passes through the owner, a designer and a project manager, and the history lives in three phones and two inboxes.

Then the real work starts, and the problems get bigger:

  • Decisions get lost. Cabinet door style, countertop edge, tile layout, hardware finish. They're agreed on in a text, a showroom visit or a hallway conversation, and someone orders the wrong thing.
  • Change orders slip. The client asks to move a wall or upgrade the vanity mid-project. If it isn't written, priced and signed before the work happens, it's a margin leak or a dispute.
  • Subcontractors need coordination. Electricians, plumbers, tile setters and countertop fabricators all depend on the job being ready when they arrive.
  • Clients feel ignored. Homeowners living through a remodel want updates. Silence for a week while you wait on an inspection reads as neglect, even when nothing is wrong.
  • Old leads are never revisited. The bathroom prospect who said "next year" never hears from you again.

What we build for remodelers

We build the CRM to follow the whole job, usually on GoHighLevel, adapted to how your company runs.

  1. A sales pipeline for the long cycle. Inquiry, Phone Screen, Consult Booked, Site Visit, Design Agreement, Proposal Sent, Revisions, Signed and Lost, with expected start dates and budget ranges on each opportunity.
  2. A project pipeline after the signature. Selections, Permits, Ordered Materials, Scheduled, Demo, Rough-In, Inspection, Finishes, Punch List, Final Walkthrough and Closed. Each stage has a clear owner.
  3. A selections log on every project. Custom fields and file uploads for cabinets, counters, tile, fixtures and paint, with the date each choice was approved, so the order matches what the client actually picked.
  4. Change order tracking. A stage-based record of each change: requested, priced, sent, signed and billed, with automatic reminders when one is waiting on a signature.
  5. Scheduled client updates. Weekly or milestone-based update messages, with templates your project manager can adjust in a minute, so homeowners hear from you before they wonder.
  6. Subcontractor scheduling reminders. Tasks and notifications tied to project stages, so the tile setter gets a heads-up when rough-in passes inspection.
  7. A nurture track for "not yet" leads. Prospects tagged by project type and timeline, with check-ins timed to their stated start window.

How it works

  1. Map your process. We document how a project moves today, from first call to final payment, including the parts that live in someone's head.
  2. Build the pipeline. We set up both pipelines, the forms, calendars and automations to match how your team works.
  3. Migrate and train. We import active projects and past clients, then train sales, design and project staff on the screens they use.
  4. Manage month to month. Optional ongoing management keeps stages, fields and automations current as you grow.

What to look for (even if you don't hire us)

When you evaluate any CRM for a remodeling company, ask:

  • Does it support separate sales and project pipelines, or only a single lead funnel?
  • Can you attach selections, drawings and signed change orders to the project record?
  • Can project managers send client updates from their phones without logging into a desktop?
  • Is there a way to see every job's current stage in one view for the Monday meeting?
  • Can you tag lost or delayed leads by reason and follow up on a schedule?
  • Will it report close rates by lead source and project type?

FAQ

Common questions

How is a remodeling CRM different from a regular sales CRM?

A sales CRM stops at 'won.' A remodeling CRM keeps going through design, selections, permits, scheduling, construction and closeout, because that's where most of the client communication and most of the risk sit. The pipeline needs stages for the project, not just the sale.

Can a CRM track change orders?

It can track that a change order was requested, sent, signed and billed, and keep a record of each one on the project. Many remodelers pair that with their estimating or accounting software for the dollar details, and the CRM handles status and communication.

How do I keep remodeling leads warm when they aren't ready for a year?

Tag them by project type and expected timeline, then send occasional useful content, such as a recent project of the same kind or a note about booking design time, rather than weekly sales pitches. Remind your team to call when their stated start window gets close.
