CRM for Painting Contractors: Estimates, Crews & Repaint Cycles

Painting companies rarely lose work because of the brush. They lose it in the gap between the estimate request and the signed proposal, and again years later when a past customer forgets who painted their house.

The problem in painting

Most painting companies run on a phone, a notebook and a calendar that only the owner fully understands. That works until spring hits and the estimate requests stack up faster than one person can drive out and measure. A homeowner who asked for an exterior quote on Monday and hears nothing until the following week has usually booked a walkthrough with someone else.

The trade has a rhythm that a generic CRM ignores:

  • Two seasons, two kinds of work. Exteriors fill the calendar from late spring into fall, and interior work carries the crews through winter. If the system can't tell the two apart, you can't market interiors in October or line up exteriors in February.
  • Estimates by the square foot and the room. Quotes depend on measurements, surface condition, prep and number of coats. Those details live on scraps of paper and in the estimator's head.
  • Crews, not technicians. You schedule a crew of two or four for three days, not one tech for one hour. Weather moves exterior jobs, and every rain day pushes the next three.
  • Repaint cycles. A satisfied customer will need the next room, the deck stain or the full exterior again in a few years. Without a record and a reminder, that repeat job goes to whoever is advertising when they finally decide.
  • Commercial bids. Property managers and general contractors want a bid by a deadline, then go quiet for weeks. Those need a different pipeline than a homeowner choosing between two quotes.

What we build for painting contractors

We set up the CRM around how a painting job actually moves. Usually that's GoHighLevel, though we'll recommend what fits.

  1. A residential estimate pipeline. Stages such as New Request, Walkthrough Booked, Estimate Sent, Follow-Up, Won, Scheduled, In Progress, Complete and Paid. Every lead from your site, phone and Facebook lands in New Request automatically.
  2. A separate commercial bid pipeline. Walkthrough, Bid Submitted, Awaiting Award, Awarded and Lost, with the bid due date and award date stored on each opportunity so nothing slips past a deadline.
  3. Walkthrough booking with reminders. Customers pick an estimate slot from your calendar, get a confirmation and a reminder the day before, and can reschedule without calling the office.
  4. Job records that remember the paint. Custom fields for interior or exterior, square footage, rooms, product, color codes and sheen, so the touch-up call three years from now takes thirty seconds.
  5. Crew scheduling views. Jobs assigned to crews on a shared calendar, with a simple way to flag weather delays and notify the customers whose start dates moved.
  6. A shared team inbox. Calls, texts, form fills and Facebook messages in one place, so the person answering the phone can see the whole conversation history.
  7. Repaint and seasonal reminders. Tagged past customers get a check-in timed to the slow season, by job type, instead of one generic "we miss you" blast.

How it works

  1. Map your process. We walk through how a request becomes a painted house today, including the parts that run through your personal texts.
  2. Build the pipeline. We configure the stages, forms, calendars and automations to match that process, residential and commercial separately.
  3. Migrate and train. We bring in your existing customer list and past jobs, then train whoever books estimates and schedules crews on the screens they'll use daily.
  4. Manage month to month. If you want ongoing management, we keep the pipelines clean and adjust them as you add crews or services.

What to look for (even if you don't hire us)

Whoever sets up your CRM, ask these questions before you sign:

  • Can it keep residential estimates and commercial bids in separate pipelines with different follow-up timing?
  • Can you store color, product and sheen on the customer record, not just in a notes field?
  • Does the calendar handle multi-day crew bookings, or only single appointments?
  • Can you message every customer on a rescheduled job in one step when weather moves the schedule?
  • Can you filter past customers by job type and completion date for repaint campaigns?
  • Will you get a simple report showing estimates sent, estimates won and where each lead came from?
  • Who fixes it when an automation breaks in the middle of May?

If a vendor can't answer those plainly, the system will end up as one more place nobody checks. Our CRM checklist for contractors goes deeper on each point.

FAQ

Common questions

Can a CRM handle both residential repaints and commercial bids?

Yes. The cleanest setup uses two pipelines: a short one for residential estimates that close in days or weeks, and a longer one for commercial bids that move through walkthroughs, bid submission and award dates. Each gets its own follow-up timing.

How do I keep track of which colors and products went on a customer's house?

Store them in custom fields on the contact or job record: brand, product line, color name and code, sheen and the room or surface. When the customer calls back for touch-ups or the next room, anyone in the office can pull it up in seconds.

When should a painting CRM remind past customers to book again?

Base it on the surface. Interior rooms and high-traffic trim tend to need attention sooner than a full exterior, so set separate reminders by job type and send them ahead of your slow season so the work lands when your crews need it.
